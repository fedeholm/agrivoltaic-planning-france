# Section 6.2: Soil Health and Hydrology

## Introduction

Agrivoltaic design and management affect soil structure, water movement, vegetation, and agricultural access. Panels change light, wind, and the distribution of rainfall; foundations, tracks, and construction traffic can disturb or compact soil. The direction and scale of these effects depend on site conditions, system layout, weather, and farm practices.

The project should protect the site's existing soil functions and water pathways, while defining measurable objectives suited to its agricultural use. These objectives should be based on a documented baseline rather than assumed benefits. This section is a practical planning guide, not legal advice; confirm project-specific requirements with qualified soil and water specialists and the relevant French authorities.

---

## 6.2.1 How Agrivoltaics Affect Soil and Water

### Soil moisture and evapotranspiration

Shade and reduced wind can lower evaporative demand in some locations, while panel edges can receive concentrated rainfall and the spaces between rows can remain more exposed. Agrivoltaic soil-moisture responses therefore vary across a site and over the season. In a semi-arid field experiment, plants beneath panels experienced less heat and water stress and used water more efficiently; these results do not establish that every soil, climate, crop, or system will retain more moisture. See the open-access [PLOS ONE agrivoltaics field study](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0203256).

Measure moisture in representative positions, such as beneath panels, at drip edges, between rows, and in an unpanelled reference area where feasible. Interpret readings alongside rainfall, irrigation, crop stage, soil type, and panel configuration.

### Infiltration, runoff, and erosion

Rainfall interception and discharge at panel edges, compacted tracks, bare ground, and altered drainage can change where water infiltrates and where it flows. Concentrated flow may cause rilling, sediment movement, or scour at outlets; intact vegetation and stable soil aggregates help slow water and limit erosion. Neither increased nor decreased runoff should be presumed without considering the site's soils, slope, design, and storm conditions.

For French soil information, consult the [GIS Sol soil data portal](https://www.gissol.fr/) and the European Commission Joint Research Centre's [European Soil Data Centre erosion resources](https://esdac.jrc.ec.europa.eu/themes/soil-erosion). These support screening and context; site investigation is still needed for project design.

### Compaction and soil structure

Repeated machinery traffic, especially on wet or weakly bearing soils, can reduce pore space, restrict roots, and slow infiltration. Compaction may persist after construction and can increase ponding, runoff, and erosion. Construction access, foundations, trenching, and routine maintenance should therefore be planned to minimize the area and duration of disturbance.

## 6.2.2 Soil Health Objectives

Set a small number of site-specific objectives before construction and assign responsibility for achieving and checking them. Depending on the baseline and agricultural system, objectives may include:

- Retain soil structure and rooting conditions in productive areas.
- Avoid persistent compaction and maintain or improve infiltration relative to baseline.
- Preserve topsoil and its biological and physical functions wherever earthworks are required.
- Keep soil covered where practicable and limit erosion and sediment transport.
- Maintain existing drainage and avoid creating harmful ponding or concentrated discharge.
- Protect watercourses, ditches, wetlands, and neighboring land from sediment-laden runoff.
- Track soil moisture and water use where these are material to crop or pasture performance.

The EU's [Soil Strategy for 2030](https://environment.ec.europa.eu/topics/soil-and-land/soil-strategy_en) frames healthy soils as important to food production, water regulation, and ecosystem function. Use its principles as context, not as a substitute for applicable French requirements or site-specific targets.

## 6.2.3 Design Measures

### Let soil and water inform the layout

Map soil types and depth, slope, drainage, existing erosion, wet areas, watercourses, ditches, and likely runoff pathways before fixing panel rows, access routes, or drainage works. Avoid sensitive or unstable areas where feasible. Check how foundations, cables, tracks, and panel drip lines may change flow paths, including during intense rainfall. The [French GIS Sol portal](https://www.gissol.fr/) and [Eaufrance](https://www.eaufrance.fr/) provide national soil and water information to inform initial screening. For geological and groundwater context, consult the BRGM's [InfoTerre portal](https://infoterre.brgm.fr/).

### Protect topsoil and soil structure

Minimize grading and soil stripping. Where excavation is necessary, follow a soil-handling plan: separate topsoil from subsoil, keep stockpiles out of drainage routes, protect them from erosion and compaction, and reuse the material in its intended horizon. Define equipment routes and turning areas; use lower-ground-pressure equipment where practicable and avoid trafficking saturated soil.

### Manage runoff and retain vegetation

Design drainage with the farm layout and local topography so that panel runoff is not simply transferred to a new erosion point or neighboring parcel. Where appropriate, disperse or safely convey concentrated flows, stabilize outlets, and use vegetated strips or infiltration features suited to the soil and groundwater conditions. Do not direct runoff toward watercourses, buildings, or sensitive habitats without assessment.

Maintain crop, pasture, or other suitable vegetation cover between rows and around infrastructure where compatible with farm operations, fire safety, and maintenance. Restore bare or disturbed soil promptly with locally suitable cover. Coordinate grazing, mowing, tillage, and machinery access with soil moisture and seasonal vulnerability.

The EU [Water Framework Directive](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/dir/2000/60/oj/eng) provides the framework for protecting water bodies; it does not replace project-level drainage design or French authorization checks.

## 6.2.4 Construction Controls

Construction is often the period of greatest risk to soil structure and drainage. Translate the soil and water plan into contractor instructions, drawings, and inspection records before works begin.

### Before works

- Record baseline soil, drainage, vegetation cover, and visible erosion.
- Mark sensitive soils, water features, exclusion areas, and approved traffic routes.
- Identify topsoil stockpile locations and a method for separate handling and reuse.
- Set wet-weather limits and stop-work criteria with the contractor and soil specialist.
- Install temporary runoff and sediment controls before disturbing ground.

### During and after works

- Keep heavy traffic on designated routes and avoid unnecessary passes over agricultural soil.
- Limit earthworks and trench widths; separate topsoil and subsoil where soil is excavated.
- Keep temporary and permanent drainage functional and prevent sediment leaving the work area.
- Stabilize exposed ground promptly; inspect controls after rainfall and repair failures.
- Restore ruts or compacted areas using a site-appropriate method, then re-establish vegetation.
- Record incidents, corrective actions, and any changes to drainage or access.

Controls should be adjusted to actual soil moisture and weather. Soil that is workable when dry may be damaged by the same equipment when saturated.

## 6.2.5 Monitoring and Adaptive Management

### Establish a baseline and indicators

Before construction, document conditions at representative locations and methods so results can be compared over time. Depending on project risks, record soil texture and horizons, organic matter, compaction indicators, infiltration observations, ground cover, erosion features, drainage, and soil moisture. Use comparable locations under panels, near panel edges, between rows, and outside the array where feasible.

During construction and operation, monitor indicators that relate directly to project objectives:

- Rutting, bare ground, visible compaction, and vegetation establishment.
- Rills, gullies, sediment deposits, ponding, and scour at drainage outlets.
- Soil moisture or irrigation demand in representative agricultural zones.
- Condition of tracks, ditches, drainage features, and sediment controls.
- Storm events, maintenance traffic, agricultural activities, and restoration actions.

Use repeatable methods and interpret changes in light of rainfall, crop and grazing practices, soil variability, and maintenance. Monitoring should be proportionate to site risk and any permit or funding commitments.

### Review and adapt

Inspect after significant rainfall, at key construction milestones, and periodically during early operation. If monitoring identifies a problem, determine whether the cause is panel runoff, traffic, vegetation loss, drainage blockage, or another site factor. Adjust access or wet-weather restrictions, repair erosion, improve or relocate runoff controls, restore cover, or revise vegetation and grazing management. Record the action, responsible party, and follow-up check.

## 6.2.6 French Project and Planning Context

Integrate soil and hydrology into feasibility, agricultural planning, environmental assessment, and engineering from the outset. For a French project, check the current provisions of the [agrivoltaics framework, including Article L. 314-36 of the Code de l'énergie and its implementing decree](https://www.legifrance.gouv.fr/jorf/id/JORFTEXT000049386027), alongside applicable planning and environmental procedures.

Where works may affect water, drainage, wetlands, or flood risk, screen the project under the [French Code de l'environnement](https://www.legifrance.gouv.fr/codes/texte_lc/LEGITEXT000006074220/) and consult the competent authorities on any required assessment or authorization. Use the [Water Framework Directive](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/dir/2000/60/oj/eng), the [EU Floods Directive](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/dir/2007/60/oj/eng), and current local water-planning documents as relevant context. Requirements depend on site conditions and project characteristics; this guide does not determine whether a particular project needs an authorization.

---

## 6.2.7 Project Checklist

- [ ] Map soils, slopes, drainage, water features, and runoff routes before fixing the layout.
- [ ] Establish baseline conditions and measurable, site-specific soil objectives.
- [ ] Minimize grading, traffic, and soil disturbance; protect topsoil where excavation is needed.
- [ ] Set wet-weather controls and approved routes for construction traffic.
- [ ] Design panel runoff and drainage to avoid erosion, ponding, and off-site impacts.
- [ ] Maintain or restore suitable vegetation cover and stabilize exposed soil promptly.
- [ ] Monitor compaction, soil cover, moisture, erosion, and drainage at representative locations.
- [ ] Review results after rainfall and adapt access, drainage, or vegetation management as needed.
- [ ] Confirm current French planning, environmental, and water requirements with qualified professionals and authorities.
