# Section 5.12: Case Study — Typical 1.5 MW Project Timeline

## Introduction

This case study presents an indicative development and delivery schedule for a 1.5 MW agrivoltaic project in France. Activities overlap, and actual durations depend on site conditions, environmental constraints, planning-authority processes, grid availability, financing, and procurement. Allow **36–48 months from site selection to commercial operations**.

## 5.12.1 Project Timeline

| Period | Phase | Main activities and milestones |
|---|---|---|
| **Months 0–3** | Pre-project and site selection | Identify candidate sites; complete an initial solar and agricultural feasibility assessment; begin stakeholder engagement; prepare a preliminary business case. |
| **Months 3–9** | Detailed feasibility and early financing | Complete solar, agricultural, and environmental site assessments; analyze subsidy-program eligibility; consult the planning authority before application; commission an environmental assessment; begin preliminary discussions with banks. |
| **Months 6–15** | Design and permitting | Develop detailed engineering design; submit the planning application and national subsidy applications; run competitive contractor bidding; obtain environmental approval. |
| **Months 12–18** | Financing approval and contract signature | Receive conditional subsidy approvals; secure bank financing; sign the grid-connection agreement; award construction contracts; arrange insurance. |
| **Months 15–33** | Construction | Months 15–18: foundations and site infrastructure. Months 15–28: equipment procurement and delivery. Months 18–33: agricultural-system installation. Months 21–30: panel installation and electrical work. Months 30–33: commissioning setup. |
| **Months 33–36** | Commissioning and handover | Complete electrical and safety testing; synchronize and connect to the grid; verify performance; train staff; activate the feed-in tariff (FIT) and hand over the project. |
| **Month 36 onward** | Operations | Begin full commercial operation and positive cash-flow generation; optimize performance; conduct routine maintenance and annual reporting. |

### Schedule management

The schedule is indicative rather than a guarantee. Track permitting, grid connection, subsidy decisions, long-lead equipment, and agricultural installation as critical interfaces. Update the project schedule when approvals, procurement dates, or site conditions change, and preserve contingency for delays that affect construction sequencing or commissioning.

## 5.14 Success Metrics and KPIs

Set a baseline, owner, measurement frequency, and reporting method for each KPI before construction. Compare operational results with approved forecasts and agricultural baselines.

### Construction phase

| KPI | Measurement or target |
|---|---|
| Schedule adherence | Percentage of milestones completed on time |
| Budget control | Variance from the approved budget |
| Safety record | Target: zero incidents |
| Quality | Percentage of punch-list items resolved |
| Stakeholder satisfaction | Feedback scores from relevant stakeholders |

### Operational phase

| KPI | Measurement or target |
|---|---|
| Energy production | Actual generation versus forecast |
| Agricultural yield | Actual yield versus baseline |
| System uptime | Target: greater than 99% |
| Financial performance | Actual results versus budget |
| Return on investment | Progress against the target internal rate of return (IRR) |
| Employee and operator satisfaction | Feedback from employees and operators |
| Environmental and biodiversity outcomes | Results against the project monitoring plan |

## 5.15 Decommissioning and End-of-Life Planning

### 5.15.1 Lifecycle planning (months 12–36 and ongoing)

- Assess panel recyclability and manufacturer take-back programs during procurement.
- Plan for inverter replacement and compliant disposal on an indicative 10–15-year cycle.
- Identify any land-restoration obligations and reflect them in project agreements.
- Consider the expected life of the agricultural system and how its eventual phase-out will be managed.
- Maintain and periodically update the decommissioning plan as equipment, contracts, and regulatory obligations change.

### 5.15.2 Decommissioning strategy (year 25 onward)

- Plan panel recycling and material recovery.
- Estimate equipment salvage value.
- Assess and budget for environmental restoration, if required.
- Define the intended land use after decommissioning.
- Establish financial reserves for decommissioning costs; the indicative planning range is **€50,000–€150,000**.

Validate end-of-life assumptions and reserve levels against the final system design, contractual obligations, and applicable requirements.
