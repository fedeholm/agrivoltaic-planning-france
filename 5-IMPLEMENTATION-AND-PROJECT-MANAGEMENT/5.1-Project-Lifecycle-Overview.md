# Section 5.1: Project Lifecycle Overview

## Introduction

An agrivoltaic project in France is a complex, multi-year undertaking that integrates solar energy generation with agricultural production. The project lifecycle spans from initial concept through operational maintenance, typically requiring 36–48 months from site selection to full commercial operation. This section provides a comprehensive overview of the eight major phases, key activities, milestones, and decision points that define successful agrivoltaic project delivery.

Understanding and planning for each phase—including dependencies, resource requirements, and risk factors—is critical to ensuring projects stay on schedule, within budget, and meet both energy and agricultural performance targets.

---

## Overview of the Eight Project Phases

### **Phase 1: Pre-Project Phase (Months 0–3)**

**Duration:** 3 months  
**Key Objective:** Identify candidate sites and confirm preliminary viability

#### Major Activities
- Identify and evaluate multiple potential sites (5–10 candidates)
- Conduct initial solar resource screening (using freely available tools like PVGIS)
- Perform preliminary agricultural land suitability assessment
- Engage with landowners/farmers for permission to conduct studies
- Establish initial project governance and decision-making structure
- Preliminary cost-benefit screening

#### Deliverables
- Site shortlist (3–5 sites for further study)
- Preliminary project brief and objectives
- Landowner/stakeholder engagement plan
- Initial budget estimate (±50% accuracy)

#### Key Stakeholders
- Project sponsor/investor
- Site owner/farmer
- Project manager (part-time)
- Financial advisor (if required)

#### Success Criteria
- At least 2–3 viable sites identified for detailed study
- Landowner agreements in principle (letter of intent)
- Stakeholder consensus on project objectives

---

### **Phase 2: Feasibility & Planning Phase (Months 3–12)**

**Duration:** 9 months  
**Key Objective:** Conduct detailed site assessment and produce comprehensive feasibility report

#### Major Activities
- Full solar resource assessment (irradiance data, shade analysis, performance modeling)
- Detailed agricultural land capability and market analysis
- Environmental baseline assessment (biodiversity, hydrology, soil)
- Grid connection feasibility study (technical requirements and estimated costs)
- Permitting pathway analysis (planning authority pre-consultation)
- Environmental impact assessment scoping
- Detailed financial modeling and subsidy program eligibility analysis
- Stakeholder engagement and community consultation

#### Deliverables
- Comprehensive feasibility report (50–100 pages)
- Solar generation model (annual yield prediction with confidence intervals)
- Agricultural production forecast
- Environmental baseline report
- Grid connection feasibility letter
- Financial model with base case, optimistic, and pessimistic scenarios
- Subsidy program application roadmap

#### Key Stakeholders
- Feasibility consultant (external)
- Solar engineer
- Agricultural advisor
- Environmental specialist
- Grid connection operator
- Local planning authority
- Financial advisor
- Farmer/landowner

#### Success Criteria
- Go/No-Go decision made with clear rationale
- Stakeholder consensus achieved (farmer, planning authority, grid operator)
- Financing pathway identified (subsidy programs, banks)
- Key risks identified and mitigation strategies proposed

---

### **Phase 3: Financing & Approvals Phase (Months 6–18)**

**Duration:** 12 months (overlaps with Phase 2)  
**Key Objective:** Secure funding commitments and obtain regulatory approvals

#### Major Activities
- Submit applications for national and EU subsidy programs
- Conduct bank feasibility discussions and pre-qualification
- Prepare planning permission application
- Submit planning application to local authority
- Obtain environmental impact assessment (EIA) screening decision
- Engage with grid connection operator (formal application and technical studies)
- Conduct detailed environmental assessment (if required)
- Complete public consultation process (if required)
- Negotiate financing agreements with banks/lenders
- Obtain planning permission
- Receive subsidy conditional approval letters
- Finalize grid connection agreement

#### Deliverables
- Subsidy conditional approval letters (with conditions attached)
- Bank loan commitment letter (conditional on planning permission)
- Planning permission certificate
- Grid connection agreement (signed)
- Environmental approval/screening certificate
- Business plan and financial documentation
- Insurance quotes

#### Key Stakeholders
- Subsidy program administrators (ADEME, Banque des Territoires, etc.)
- Bank/lender
- Planning authority
- Environmental regulator (DREAL)
- Grid operator (Enedis or local distributor)
- Project manager
- Investor/sponsor

#### Success Criteria
- Subsidy conditional approvals received (with realistic conditions)
- Bank financing committed (before final disbursement conditions)
- Planning permission granted
- Grid connection agreement signed
- All regulatory approvals in place
- Financial close: Investor commits capital; bank releases first tranche

---

### **Phase 4: Design & Engineering Phase (Months 9–15)**

**Duration:** 6–9 months (overlaps with Phases 2 and 3)  
**Key Objective:** Produce detailed design, specifications, and engineering documentation

#### Major Activities
- Detailed solar system design (array layout, inverter sizing, cabling, grounding, protection)
- Structural and geotechnical engineering (foundation design, wind load analysis, soil bearing)
- Electrical design (single-line diagrams, load flow analysis, protection coordination)
- Agricultural system design (crop layout, irrigation, equipment access, storage)
- Environmental and biodiversity design (habitat creation, stormwater management, landscaping)
- Create detailed construction and electrical drawings
- Develop technical specifications for all major equipment and systems
- Conduct design reviews and value engineering
- Refine project budget to ±10–15% accuracy
- Develop operation and maintenance manual framework

#### Deliverables
- Complete design drawings (site plan, electrical, structural, agricultural, environmental)
- Technical specifications (solar equipment, mounting, electrical, agricultural systems)
- Bill of materials (BOM) for all major components
- Detailed budget breakdown by work package
- Contingency analysis (10–15% contingency included)
- Design review report and approval sign-offs
- O&M manual framework and templates

#### Key Stakeholders
- Lead design engineer (internal or external)
- Electrical engineer
- Structural/civil engineer
- Agricultural system designer
- Environmental designer
- Quantity surveyor/cost estimator
- Grid operator (for electrical design coordination)
- Contractor (for constructability input)

#### Success Criteria
- All drawings and specifications complete and approved
- Budget finalized within ±10–15% accuracy
- Design approved by all relevant authorities (grid operator, planning authority)
- Construction ready to proceed (contractor can bid from designs)
- Contingency planning established

---

### **Phase 5: Procurement & Contracting Phase (Months 12–18)**

**Duration:** 6–12 months (overlaps with Phases 3 and 4)  
**Key Objective:** Award contracts and secure equipment and services

#### Major Activities
- Identify and pre-qualify contractors and equipment suppliers
- Develop tender documents (technical specs, contract terms, evaluation criteria)
- Conduct competitive bidding (typically 3–5 bids per major contract)
- Evaluate bids (price, technical compliance, delivery schedule, contractor quality)
- Award contracts to selected suppliers/contractors
- Issue purchase orders and equipment delivery schedules
- Arrange logistics and site storage for equipment
- Finalize insurance and performance bonds
- Conduct pre-mobilization meetings

#### Deliverables
- Signed EPC (Engineering, Procurement, Construction) contract
- Signed supplier purchase orders for solar panels, inverters, transformers
- Signed agricultural system installation contract
- Signed O&M service contracts (if outsourced)
- Insurance certificates and performance bonds
- Detailed construction schedule and logistics plan
- Pre-mobilization meeting minutes

#### Key Stakeholders
- Procurement manager
- Finance manager
- Contractors (EPC, agricultural systems)
- Equipment suppliers (panels, inverters, mounting, electrical)
- Insurance broker
- Lender (for contract approval)

#### Success Criteria
- All major contracts awarded at competitive prices
- Total contract value within approved budget
- Contractor quality and experience verified
- Equipment delivery schedules confirmed (with long lead items ordered first)
- Insurance and performance bonds in place
- Contractor mobilization ready

---

### **Phase 6: Construction & Installation Phase (Months 15–36)**

**Duration:** 18–24 months  
**Key Objective:** Physically construct solar and agricultural systems on site

#### Major Activities
- Site mobilization (site office, temporary facilities, safety systems)
- Site preparation (clearing, grading, access roads, utility trenching)
- Foundation and structural work (posts, concrete pads, mounting structure assembly)
- Electrical infrastructure (trenching, cabling, transformer pad, substation)
- Solar panel installation and electrical connections
- Inverter and transformer installation and testing
- Agricultural infrastructure (irrigation systems, storage facilities, livestock areas)
- Environmental features (hedgerows, habitat areas, stormwater management)
- Quality inspections and testing at each milestone
- Daily progress reporting and safety monitoring
- Change management and cost control

#### Deliverables
- Constructed solar array (panels mounted and electrically connected)
- Installed electrical infrastructure (transformers, cabling, protection systems)
- Constructed agricultural infrastructure (irrigation, storage, access roads)
- Installed environmental features (habitat, landscaping)
- Inspection reports and testing certificates
- Weekly and monthly progress reports
- Photographic documentation

#### Key Stakeholders
- Site construction manager
- EPC contractor and subcontractors
- Agricultural system contractor
- Quality assurance/quality control (QA/QC) inspector
- Health and safety officer
- Lender/investor (site supervision)
- Planning authority (periodic inspections)
- Environmental compliance officer

#### Success Criteria
- All physical work completed on schedule (or with justified delays and recovery plans)
- Quality standards met (zero critical defects; all minor defects resolved)
- Budget maintained (change orders minimized and approved)
- Safety record achieved (zero lost-time incidents)
- Environmental protection measures implemented as designed

---

### **Phase 7: Commissioning & Testing Phase (Months 33–36)**

**Duration:** 3–6 months (overlaps with end of construction)  
**Key Objective:** Verify all systems are operational and meet performance specifications

#### Major Activities
- Electrical safety testing (insulation resistance, grounding, continuity)
- Inverter programming, configuration, and functional testing
- Transformer and protection system testing
- Grid synchronization testing and final grid connection
- Solar performance testing (full array power output, efficiency)
- Monitoring system installation, calibration, and activation
- Agricultural system testing (irrigation flow rates, equipment operation)
- Environmental monitoring baseline setup (biodiversity surveys, soil testing)
- Staff training (40–80 hours for operators and maintenance teams)
- Documentation handover (manuals, drawings, warranties, records)
- 30-day operational test period (all systems running at design conditions)
- Final inspections and sign-offs by relevant authorities
- FIT contract activation and monitoring setup

#### Deliverables
- Electrical test reports and safety certificates
- Performance testing report (solar output, system efficiency)
- Grid connection final approval letter
- Training records and certification of operators
- Monitoring system activation report
- Environmental baseline monitoring report
- Operation and maintenance manuals (complete)
- Warranty and guarantee documentation
- Insurance activation confirmation
- FIT contract activation letter

#### Key Stakeholders
- Commissioning engineer
- Electrical inspector
- Grid operator (final connection)
- Monitoring system provider
- Operator and maintenance staff
- Environmental monitoring specialist
- Lender/investor
- Insurance provider

#### Success Criteria
- All systems commissioned and tested successfully
- Performance verified to meet or exceed design specifications
- Zero critical defects at handover
- All staff trained and competent
- FIT contract activated and payment process established
- Insurance activated
- Monitoring systems operational

---

### **Phase 8: Operation & Maintenance Phase (Year 1 – Year 25+)**

**Duration:** 25 years (entire project lifetime)  
**Key Objective:** Maximize energy and agricultural production while minimizing operational costs

#### Major Activities
- Daily operational monitoring (real-time dashboard, alert response)
- Routine maintenance (panel cleaning, equipment inspections, software updates)
- Preventive maintenance (inverter service, component replacement per schedule)
- Corrective maintenance (equipment failures, repairs, replacements)
- Agricultural management (crop rotation, pest/disease management, harvesting)
- Soil health monitoring (annual testing, amendment applications)
- Biodiversity monitoring (annual wildlife surveys, habitat maintenance)
- Performance tracking and optimization
- Financial management and reporting
- Regulatory compliance (subsidy reporting, tax filings, environmental reporting)
- Insurance management and claims
- Staff management and training

#### Deliverables
- Monthly operational reports
- Annual performance reports (energy production vs. forecast)
- Annual maintenance reports
- Annual financial statements and tax reports
- Subsidy compliance reports (to relevant agencies)
- Environmental monitoring reports
- Insurance certificates and updates

#### Key Stakeholders
- Site operations manager
- Maintenance technician(s)
- Agricultural manager/farmer
- Monitoring system provider
- Equipment suppliers (for warranty support)
- Insurance provider
- Subsidy program administrators
- Environmental regulator (for reporting)
- Lender/investor (for performance tracking)

#### Success Criteria
- Sustained energy production within ±5% of long-term forecast
- Agricultural yield meets or exceeds baseline expectations
- System availability >99% (minimal unscheduled downtime)
- Operating costs within budget
- Positive cash flow and financial returns achieved
- Environmental/biodiversity targets met
- Safe operation record (zero safety incidents)
- Stakeholder satisfaction maintained

## Key Milestones and Decision Gates

Each phase concludes with a major decision point (gate) where stakeholders assess whether to proceed to the next phase.

| **Phase** | **Duration** | **End Milestone** | **Go/No-Go Decision** | **Typical Condition** |
|-----------|--------------|------|---|---|
| 1. Pre-Project | 0–3 mo | Sites identified | Proceed to feasibility? | 2–3 sites viable |
| 2. Feasibility | 3–12 mo | Feasibility report | Proceed to financing? | IRR >8%; farmer support |
| 3. Financing | 6–18 mo | Financing close | Proceed to construction? | Funding secured; permits approved |
| 4. Design | 9–15 mo | Final design drawings | Proceed to construction? | Design approved; budget confirmed |
| 5. Procurement | 12–18 mo | Contracts awarded | Proceed to construction? | All contracts signed; delivery confirmed |
| 6. Construction | 15–36 mo | Physical completion | Proceed to commissioning? | Handover inspection passed |
| 7. Commissioning | 33–36 mo | Systems operational | Accept handover? | 30-day test passed; FIT activated |
| 8. Operations | 36+ mo | Positive cash flow | Continue? | Performance targets met |

## Critical Path and Schedule Dependencies

### Phase Dependencies (Sequential Requirements)

```
Phase 1 (Pre-Project) → Decision Gate → Phase 2 (Feasibility)
    ↓
    └─→ Phase 3 (Financing & Approvals) [starts month 6, parallel with Phase 2]
         ├─→ Grid connection application (month 6)
         ├─→ Planning application (month 9)
         ├─→ Subsidy applications (month 6–12)
         ├─→ Bank financing (month 6–18)
         └─→ Decision Gate → Financial Close (month 18)

Phase 2 (Feasibility) → Decision Gate → Phase 4 (Design & Engineering) [starts month 9]
    └─→ Phase 5 (Procurement) [starts month 12, after Phase 4 designs ready]
         └─→ Decision Gate → Phase 6 (Construction) [starts month 15]
             └─→ Phase 7 (Commissioning) [starts month 33, parallel with construction completion]
                 └─→ Decision Gate → Phase 8 (Operations) [starts month 36]

**Critical Path:** Pre-Project → Feasibility → Financing Approval → Design → Procurement → Construction → Commissioning → Operations
```

### Schedule Compression Opportunities

Certain activities can be performed in parallel to reduce overall project duration:
- **Financing & Permitting (Parallel):** Design can begin while financing is still being negotiated, compressing timeline by 2–3 months
- **Procurement (Early Orders):** Long lead-time items (solar panels, inverters, transformers) can be ordered early (month 12–15) if funding is confirmed
- **Commissioning (Overlap):** Final system testing and staff training can begin while construction is still wrapping up (final 10–20% of work)

**Best-case minimum duration:** 30–33 months (aggressive timeline with optimal sequencing)  
**Typical duration:** 36–42 months (realistic timeline with standard sequencing)  
**Conservative/high-risk scenario:** 42–48 months (delays in permitting, financing, or construction)

## Risk and Contingency Planning

### Contingency Reserves by Phase

| **Phase** | **Contingency %** | **Rationale** |
|-----------|---|---|
| Pre-Project & Feasibility | 0% | Preliminary stage; low commitment |
| Financing & Design | 10–15% | Design uncertainty; subsidy risks |
| Procurement & Construction | 10–15% | Execution risks; supply chain uncertainties |
| Commissioning & Operations | 5% | Lower risk; systems tested and proven |

### Risk Categories and Mitigation Strategies

**Schedule Risks:**
- Permitting delays (6–18 month risk): Engage planning authority early (pre-application consultation)
- Supply chain delays (2–4 month risk): Order long lead items early; identify alternate suppliers
- Construction delays (2–4 week risk): Weather contingencies; experienced contractor selection
- Financing delays (2–6 month risk): Multiple funding sources; early bank engagement

**Cost Risks:**
- Material price inflation (3–5% annual risk): Lock in long lead item pricing early; fixed-price contracts
- Labor cost increases (2–3% annual risk): Competitive bidding; productivity improvements
- Regulatory/compliance changes (variable): Monitor regulatory landscape; maintain flexibility in design

**Technical Risks:**
- Performance shortfalls (solar: <5%; agricultural: <10%): Conservative modeling; performance guarantees in contracts
- Grid connection delays: Early application and coordination with grid operator
- Equipment failures: Vendor warranties; performance bonds; redundant systems where critical

## Success Factors for Each Phase

### Phase 1: Pre-Project
- ✓ Clear, realistic project objectives defined
- ✓ Multiple sites identified and screened
- ✓ Landowner/farmer buy-in and collaboration established
- ✓ Preliminary subsidy and financing pathways identified

### Phase 2: Feasibility
- ✓ High-quality technical and economic analysis
- ✓ Transparent communication of risks and assumptions
- ✓ Stakeholder consensus on project viability
- ✓ Clear go/no-go decision criteria established

### Phase 3: Financing & Approvals
- ✓ Subsidy applications prepared thoroughly and submitted on time
- ✓ Bank relationship established early; financing secured before permitting complete
- ✓ Planning authority engaged proactively; application prepared comprehensively
- ✓ Environmental assessment completed to high standards

### Phase 4: Design & Engineering
- ✓ Experienced, qualified design team assembled
- ✓ Design reviews conducted with all stakeholders
- ✓ Designs optimized for constructability, O&M, and cost
- ✓ Detailed specifications and drawings ready for contractor bidding

### Phase 5: Procurement & Contracting
- ✓ Competitive bidding conducted fairly; best value selected
- ✓ Contractor quality and experience verified
- ✓ Contracts clearly define scope, schedule, costs, and quality standards
- ✓ Long lead items ordered early; delivery schedules confirmed

### Phase 6: Construction
- ✓ Experienced site management and supervision
- ✓ Daily safety and quality monitoring
- ✓ Proactive change management and cost control
- ✓ Regular communication with stakeholders and local community

### Phase 7: Commissioning
- ✓ Comprehensive testing protocols executed thoroughly
- ✓ Staff training delivered comprehensively
- ✓ Documentation complete and accessible
- ✓ Defect remediation timely and complete

### Phase 8: Operations
- ✓ Competent, motivated operations and maintenance team
- ✓ Routine maintenance performed on schedule
- ✓ Performance monitoring and optimization
- ✓ Financial management and regulatory compliance maintained

## Conclusion

The agrivoltaic project lifecycle is a structured, multi-phase process that requires careful planning, execution, and monitoring. Success depends on understanding the dependencies between phases, managing resources effectively, and maintaining stakeholder alignment throughout. By following this framework—with clear milestones, decision gates, and contingency planning—developers can navigate the complexities of integrating solar energy and agricultural production while delivering financial returns, environmental benefits, and community acceptance.
