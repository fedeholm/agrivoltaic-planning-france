# Section 5.6: Construction and Installation

## Introduction

The Construction and Installation phase converts the approved design into an operating agrivoltaic facility. It typically runs from months 15–36, although the exact duration depends on project scale, site conditions, permitting, grid-connection requirements, weather, agricultural calendars, and supply-chain performance.

Construction must deliver two integrated systems:

- A safe, reliable, and grid-compliant solar PV system
- A practical agricultural system that remains accessible, productive, and maintainable

Construction planning should therefore treat agricultural access, soil protection, irrigation, biodiversity measures, and community relations as core work packages rather than secondary activities.

> **Important:** Construction work must be directed and supervised by qualified contractors and professionals in accordance with approved designs, applicable French requirements, manufacturer instructions, and site-specific health, safety, environmental, and agricultural conditions.

---

## 5.6.1 Pre-Construction Planning

### Construction readiness review

Before mobilization, the project manager should confirm that the following conditions are satisfied:

- [ ] Construction contracts are signed and insurance is active
- [ ] Required permits and approvals are in force
- [ ] Grid-connection requirements are confirmed
- [ ] Detailed construction drawings are approved for issue
- [ ] Long-lead equipment is ordered and delivery dates are confirmed
- [ ] Land access and temporary construction rights are documented
- [ ] Construction schedule and budget baseline are approved
- [ ] Health, safety, and environmental plans are approved
- [ ] Agricultural operating calendar has been coordinated with the farmer
- [ ] Emergency contacts and escalation procedures are established
- [ ] Community communication arrangements are active

### Site mobilization and preparation

Mobilization establishes the temporary facilities and controls needed to begin work safely and efficiently.

Typical activities include:

- Establishing the site entrance and security controls
- Installing temporary offices, welfare facilities, storage, and first-aid equipment
- Setting up temporary power, water, lighting, and communications
- Marking parcel boundaries, exclusion zones, ecological buffers, and access routes
- Surveying and setting out the approved layout
- Establishing material delivery and laydown areas
- Installing temporary drainage and erosion controls
- Recording pre-construction site conditions with photographs and surveys
- Confirming locations of existing utilities, drains, wells, and farm infrastructure
- Installing signage and traffic-control measures

The site should be organized to minimize unnecessary traffic across productive agricultural areas and to preserve access for ongoing farm operations.

### Workforce recruitment and training

The construction team should include competent personnel for:

- Project and construction management
- Civil works and earthworks
- Piling, foundations, and structural assembly
- Electrical installation and testing
- Agricultural infrastructure
- Environmental compliance
- Quality assurance and document control
- Health and safety
- Logistics and traffic management

Before starting work, all personnel and subcontractors should receive induction covering:

- Site rules and boundaries
- Safe systems of work
- Personal protective equipment
- Emergency response and evacuation
- Electrical and lifting hazards
- Working near agricultural operations and livestock
- Soil, water, and biodiversity protection
- Incident and near-miss reporting
- Traffic routes and speed limits
- Community and landowner conduct

Training records should be maintained and refreshed when work methods, personnel, or site conditions change.

### Construction schedule and milestones

The baseline schedule should show:

- Design release dates
- Procurement and delivery dates
- Site mobilization
- Civil and foundation works
- Structural installation
- PV module installation
- Electrical works
- Agricultural infrastructure
- Environmental measures
- Grid-connection works
- Testing and commissioning
- Handover and documentation

Key milestones should be measurable and linked to payment, inspection, and decision gates. Examples include:

1. Site mobilization complete
2. Access roads and temporary drainage operational
3. Foundation installation complete
4. Mounting structures complete
5. PV modules installed
6. Electrical balance of system complete
7. Agricultural infrastructure complete
8. Environmental works complete
9. Mechanical completion achieved
10. Ready for energization
11. Commissioning complete
12. Handover accepted

### Traffic management and site access

A traffic management plan should define:

- Approved delivery routes
- Vehicle size and weight restrictions
- Site entrance and exit arrangements
- One-way systems and passing areas
- Pedestrian and vehicle segregation
- Speed limits
- Reversing and banksman procedures
- Emergency access
- Temporary road closures or diversions
- Delivery booking and communication procedures
- Protection of farm access during planting, spraying, and harvest

Heavy vehicles should use designated routes and avoid wet or vulnerable soils whenever possible. Temporary access roads should be designed for the expected loads and removed or restored where they are not required during operations.

### Environmental protection measures

Before construction starts, implement the approved construction environmental management plan. Measures may include:

- Marking no-go areas and ecological buffers
- Protecting hedgerows, trees, wetlands, ponds, and watercourses
- Restricting vegetation clearance to approved areas and periods
- Controlling dust, noise, vibration, and lighting
- Preventing fuel, oil, concrete, and chemical pollution
- Managing runoff, sediment, and erosion
- Separating and managing construction waste
- Controlling invasive species and contaminated soil
- Protecting nesting, breeding, or hibernation areas
- Restoring temporarily disturbed land

Environmental responsibilities should be assigned to named personnel, with inspections and corrective actions recorded.

### Community communication plan

The project manager should provide clear, timely information to affected residents, the municipality, the farmer, neighbouring land users, and relevant authorities.

The communication plan should include:

- Construction start notification
- Work hours and expected duration
- Delivery and traffic information
- Contact details for complaints and urgent issues
- Planned noisy or disruptive activities
- Temporary access restrictions
- Environmental incidents and corrective actions
- Progress updates at agreed intervals
- Handover and operational-start communication

Complaints should be logged, investigated, assigned to an owner, and closed with a documented response.

## 5.6.2 Foundation and Infrastructure

### Site preparation and grading

Site preparation should be limited to the minimum area required for safe construction and long-term operation.

Typical activities include:

- Surveying and setting out panel rows, roads, equipment, and exclusion zones
- Removing only approved vegetation or obstacles
- Stripping and storing topsoil separately where grading is required
- Preparing access and crane areas
- Managing unsuitable or contaminated material
- Minimizing cut-and-fill operations
- Protecting existing drainage and irrigation infrastructure
- Restoring compacted or disturbed areas after construction

Topsoil should not be mixed with subsoil or construction waste. Stockpiles should be protected from erosion and clearly identified for later reuse.

### Foundation installation

Foundation work should follow the approved structural and geotechnical design.

Possible foundation systems include:

- Driven steel posts
- Screw piles
- Concrete pads or footings
- Micropiles
- Anchored foundations
- Site-specific hybrid systems

Construction controls should include:

- Survey verification before installation
- Confirmation of pile or footing locations
- Installation depth and verticality records
- Torque, pull-out, or refusal records where applicable
- Concrete mix, placement, and curing records
- Inspection of reinforcement and anchor bolts
- Protection of underground utilities and drainage
- Management of vibration and noise
- Rejection and remediation procedure for defective foundations

Foundation changes should not be made informally. Any change caused by ground conditions should be reviewed by the responsible engineer and recorded through the change-control process.

### Access roads and internal roads

Roads should support construction traffic while remaining compatible with agricultural operations.

Design and construction considerations include:

- Appropriate width for delivery vehicles, tractors, and emergency access
- Turning areas and passing places
- Suitable subgrade and aggregate depth
- Drainage and crossfall
- Protection against rutting and erosion
- Separation from watercourses and sensitive habitats
- Gates and access controls
- Long-term maintenance requirements

Where temporary construction roads are not needed during operations, define restoration requirements before construction begins.

### Utility trenching

Trenching may be required for:

- DC and AC electrical cables
- Medium-voltage connections
- Irrigation mains and distribution pipes
- Water supply and drainage
- Communications and monitoring cables
- Temporary or permanent power supplies

Trenching controls should include:

- Approved route and service drawings
- Utility detection and trial pits where needed
- Safe excavation and shoring procedures
- Separation between electrical, water, and communication services
- Minimum cover and warning-tape requirements
- Protection at road and drainage crossings
- Bedding, backfill, and compaction specifications
- As-built survey before backfilling
- Photographic records of buried infrastructure

Avoid placing trenches where they would obstruct farm machinery, drainage, or future maintenance access.

### Stormwater management infrastructure

Construction should preserve or improve the site's ability to manage rainfall and runoff.

Measures may include:

- Swales and vegetated channels
- Temporary sediment basins
- Check dams and silt fences
- Cross-drains beneath access roads
- Culverts and headwalls
- Energy dissipation at outlets
- Infiltration areas
- Stabilized soil and reseeding
- Protection around panel drip lines and concentrated discharge points

Inspect stormwater controls after heavy rainfall and repair them promptly. Permanent drainage should be tested before the site is handed over.

## 5.6.3 Solar System Installation

### Mounting structure assembly

Mounting installation should follow approved drawings, manufacturer instructions, and structural tolerances.

Typical sequence:

1. Verify foundation locations and elevations.
2. Install posts, brackets, beams, and bracing.
3. Check alignment, torque, and connection security.
4. Install cable management and bonding components.
5. Confirm clearances for agricultural equipment and maintenance.
6. Record inspection results before installing modules.

Quality checks should cover:

- Post depth and verticality
- Structural alignment
- Bolt torque
- Corrosion protection and damaged coatings
- Bracing and wind-resistance components
- Sharp edges and worker-safety hazards
- Clearance from crops, livestock, roads, and irrigation
- Compatibility with module dimensions

### Panel installation and connections

PV modules should be handled, stored, and installed according to manufacturer instructions.

Controls include:

- Inspection for transport or handling damage
- Serial-number recording
- Dry and secure storage
- Protection from contamination and impact
- Correct orientation and clamping
- Correct connector matching and polarity
- Cable support and bend-radius compliance
- Avoidance of cables resting on soil or sharp edges
- Verification of module strings before energization

Modules should not be walked on or used as temporary work platforms. Damaged modules should be quarantined and assessed before installation.

### Inverter and transformer installation

Install inverters and transformers on prepared foundations with adequate access, ventilation, protection, and drainage.

Verify:

- Equipment model and rating against approved design
- Foundation dimensions and anchor positions
- Lifting and placement method
- Clearance for cooling and maintenance
- Protection from flooding, livestock, and unauthorized access
- Cable entry and sealing
- Earthing and bonding
- Fire and oil-containment measures where applicable
- Manufacturer commissioning requirements

Equipment should remain protected from moisture, dust, and unauthorized operation until it is ready for testing.

### Electrical cabling and connections

Electrical works should be installed, tested, and documented by competent personnel.

Typical activities include:

- DC string wiring and combiner connections
- DC and AC cable routing
- Cable trench installation
- Termination and gland installation
- Medium-voltage cable installation
- Switchgear and protection wiring
- Transformer connections
- Communications and control wiring
- Labelling and circuit identification

Before energization, verify:

- Insulation resistance
- Continuity
- Polarity
- Cable identification
- Torque of electrical connections
- Phase sequence
- Protective-device settings
- Cable routes and separation
- As-built records

### Safety systems

Safety systems should be installed as part of the electrical works, not as a final afterthought.

They may include:

- Protective earthing and bonding
- Lightning protection
- Surge protection
- DC and AC isolators
- Emergency-stop or remote-disconnection systems
- Warning signs and labels
- Arc-fault or fire-detection systems where specified
- Perimeter and equipment security
- Safe access and fall-protection provisions

The final design should identify isolation points and emergency procedures clearly for operators, firefighters, grid personnel, and maintenance contractors.

### Monitoring system installation

The monitoring system should collect enough data to support energy management, fault response, agricultural coordination, and environmental reporting.

Potential data points include:

- Inverter output
- DC voltage and current
- AC power and energy
- Irradiance
- Module or ambient temperature
- Wind speed and direction
- Rainfall
- Soil moisture
- Irrigation flow and pressure
- Water levels
- Weather alarms
- Grid status and curtailment
- Security and access events

Commissioning should verify sensor calibration, time synchronization, communications, data retention, alarm routing, remote access, and cybersecurity controls.

## 5.6.4 Agricultural System Installation

### Irrigation system installation and testing

Irrigation installation should be coordinated with crop calendars, soil conditions, and construction access.

Typical work includes:

- Installing pumps, filters, valves, meters, and control systems
- Installing main lines, submains, and distribution pipes
- Installing drip lines, micro-sprinklers, or approved alternatives
- Constructing or connecting storage tanks
- Installing pressure regulation and flow-control equipment
- Protecting pipes from vehicle traffic and frost where relevant
- Connecting sensors and automation systems

Testing should verify:

- Flow rate and pressure at representative outlets
- Uniformity of delivery
- Pump capacity and controls
- Leak tightness
- Filtration and flushing
- Electrical safety
- Water quality compatibility
- Emergency isolation
- Integration with the farm operating plan

Record test results and provide the operator with maintenance and winterization instructions.

### Equipment storage facilities

Storage facilities may include:

- Machinery shelters
- Tool and spare-parts stores
- Fertilizer or input storage
- Harvest storage
- Livestock equipment shelters
- Pump and irrigation control rooms
- Monitoring and communications cabinets

Facilities should provide:

- Safe access and loading areas
- Adequate ventilation
- Fire protection
- Drainage and spill control
- Security and lighting
- Separation of fuels, chemicals, food, and livestock materials
- Maintenance access
- Compatibility with farm vehicles and equipment

### Soil preparation

Construction often creates localized compaction and disturbance. Soil preparation should be based on post-construction testing rather than a generic treatment.

Possible measures include:

- Removing construction debris and stones
- Deep loosening or subsoiling where appropriate
- Replacing stored topsoil
- Applying approved amendments
- Restoring pH or nutrient balance
- Adding organic matter
- Reseeding disturbed areas
- Establishing erosion-control vegetation
- Testing compaction, infiltration, and fertility

The agricultural operator and soil specialist should approve the restoration method before crop establishment.

### Crop and livestock infrastructure

Depending on the production system, installation may include:

- Crop beds, trellises, or orchard supports
- Livestock fencing and gates
- Water troughs and shelters
- Poultry houses or mobile units
- Beehives and protective zones
- Handling pens and loading areas
- Pest-exclusion systems
- Harvest and processing areas
- Field drainage and access improvements

Infrastructure should maintain safe separation from electrical equipment and preserve maintenance and emergency access.

### Access and working-area layout

The final agricultural layout should be checked on site with the farmer or operator before handover.

Confirm:

- Machinery routes are wide and unobstructed
- Turning areas are usable under actual field conditions
- Panel posts and cables do not interfere with operations
- Irrigation can be inspected and repaired
- Harvest routes remain available
- Livestock can move safely between grazing and shelter areas
- Maintenance vehicles can access inverters, transformers, and roads
- Temporary construction areas have been restored or formally retained

A practical field test using the actual machinery is preferable to relying solely on drawings.

## 5.6.5 Construction Quality Assurance

### Quality-management plan

The contractor should maintain a quality plan that identifies:

- Applicable standards and specifications
- Inspection and test plans
- Hold points and witness points
- Responsible inspectors
- Required records and certificates
- Non-conformance process
- Material approval process
- Document-control procedures
- Handover requirements

### Daily inspection and defect logs

Daily records should include:

- Weather and site conditions
- Personnel and equipment present
- Work completed
- Deliveries received
- Inspections completed
- Tests performed
- Safety observations
- Environmental observations
- Agricultural access impacts
- Incidents and near misses
- Delays and causes
- Open defects and corrective actions

Defect logs should identify the location, description, responsible party, required remedy, due date, and close-out evidence.

### Materials testing and certification

Collect and organize certificates for:

- Concrete and reinforcement
- Structural steel and corrosion protection
- Piles, anchors, and fasteners
- Electrical cables and switchgear
- PV modules and inverters
- Transformers
- Irrigation pipes and pressure components
- Aggregate and road materials
- Soil amendments and seed mixtures

Materials should be checked against approved specifications before incorporation into the works.

### Milestone inspections and sign-offs

Use formal inspections at critical stages, including:

1. Site preparation and formation level
2. Foundation setting out
3. Foundation installation
4. Underground services before backfill
5. Mounting structure completion
6. Module installation
7. DC electrical completion
8. AC and medium-voltage completion
9. Irrigation pressure testing
10. Soil restoration
11. Environmental works
12. Mechanical completion
13. Pre-energization inspection
14. Commissioning and handover

A milestone should not be accepted until required tests, photographs, certificates, and corrective actions are complete.

### Photos and progress documentation

Photographic records should be systematic and georeferenced where practical. Capture:

- Pre-construction baseline
- Site preparation
- Foundations before concealment
- Underground utilities before backfill
- Structural and electrical installation
- Environmental protection measures
- Agricultural infrastructure
- Defects and corrective work
- Final completed condition

Store photographs with dates, locations, descriptions, and links to relevant inspection records.

### Safety audits

Safety audits should occur at a frequency proportionate to project risk. A typical programme includes:

- Daily supervisor inspections
- Weekly contractor safety reviews
- Monthly project-wide audits
- Quarterly independent or senior-management audits
- Additional audits after serious incidents or major work changes

Audit topics may include:

- Working at height
- Lifting operations
- Electrical isolation and energization
- Excavations and trenching
- Vehicle and pedestrian segregation
- Heat, cold, and weather exposure
- Personal protective equipment
- Emergency response
- Subcontractor competence
- Environmental and agricultural interfaces

Corrective actions should have named owners and deadlines, with closure verified rather than assumed.

## 5.6.6 Construction Timeline and Staffing

### Typical schedule

A commercial agrivoltaic project may require approximately 18–24 months for construction and installation. A smaller or simpler project may be completed faster, while difficult ground conditions, complex agricultural infrastructure, grid works, or seasonal environmental restrictions may extend the programme.

| Period | Main activities | Key outputs |
|---|---|---|
| Months 15–17 | Mobilization, surveys, temporary works, access preparation | Site ready for construction |
| Months 17–21 | Earthworks, roads, drainage, foundations | Civil and foundation packages progressing |
| Months 20–26 | Structural assembly and utility trenching | Mounting and underground infrastructure |
| Months 23–30 | Module, inverter, transformer, and electrical installation | Solar system mechanically and electrically complete |
| Months 24–32 | Irrigation, storage, fencing, soil restoration, agricultural infrastructure | Agricultural system ready for commissioning |
| Months 30–34 | Testing, defect correction, environmental completion | Mechanical completion |
| Months 33–36 | Energization, grid tests, performance tests, training, handover | Ready for operations |

The activities may overlap significantly. The baseline schedule should identify dependencies, float, critical-path activities, and recovery options.

### Parallel activities

Parallel working can shorten the project, but it increases coordination requirements. Examples include:

- Ordering equipment while civil works are underway
- Installing foundations in one zone while preparing another
- Constructing access roads while completing design details for later packages
- Installing irrigation outside the active foundation zone
- Performing electrical terminations while remaining modules are installed
- Completing monitoring and training before final construction close-out

Parallel work should proceed only where interfaces, safety controls, and approved information are adequate.

### Workforce levels

Workforce needs vary by scale and construction method.

Typical staffing pattern:

| Stage | Typical workforce | Main roles |
|---|---:|---|
| Mobilization and early civil works | 10–25 | Site manager, surveyors, civil crew, HSE, logistics |
| Foundations and structures | 20–60 | Piling crew, steel installers, equipment operators, QA/QC |
| Peak installation | 50–100 for large projects | Module crews, electricians, crane teams, supervisors |
| Agricultural and environmental works | 10–30 | Irrigation, fencing, soil, planting, environmental teams |
| Commissioning and handover | 10–25 | Electrical engineers, commissioning staff, operators, inspectors |

The project manager should track labor availability, subcontractor interfaces, accommodation and transport needs, and training requirements.

### Weather dependencies and contingencies

Weather can affect:

- Earthworks and road construction
- Concrete placement and curing
- Piling and structural work
- Electrical installation in wet conditions
- Soil restoration and reseeding
- Crane and lifting operations
- Agricultural access and crop calendars

Include contingency for:

- Prolonged rain and saturated soils
- Heatwaves and worker heat stress
- Frost and freezing conditions
- Wind limits for lifting and module installation
- Flooding or storm damage
- Restricted access during harvest or wet seasons

Recovery measures may include resequencing indoor or electrical work, increasing crews, extending working hours where permitted, using temporary access protection, or prioritizing critical-path activities.

### Cost tracking and change management

Construction cost control should compare:

- Original contract value
- Approved changes
- Committed costs
- Actual costs paid
- Forecast final cost
- Contingency remaining
- Potential claims and exposures

Maintain a live change register with:

- Change number and date
- Description and reason
- Initiating party
- Technical impact
- Cost impact
- Schedule impact
- Agricultural and environmental impact
- Approval status
- Implementation status

No material change should proceed without written authorization from the designated approval authority, except for documented emergency work.

### Monthly construction dashboard

A useful monthly dashboard includes:

- Overall schedule status
- Percentage complete by work package
- Critical-path status
- Cost variance and forecast final cost
- Equipment delivery status
- Safety statistics
- Quality defects and close-out rate
- Environmental incidents and corrective actions
- Agricultural disruption and access status
- Community complaints and responses
- Key decisions required from the owner
- Top risks and mitigation actions

## 5.6.7 Construction Handover Readiness

Before moving into commissioning, confirm that construction has produced a complete and safe installation.

### Mechanical completion checklist

- [ ] All structural works complete
- [ ] All PV modules installed and inspected
- [ ] Inverters, transformers, and switchgear installed
- [ ] Electrical cables terminated and labelled
- [ ] Grounding and surge protection installed
- [ ] Monitoring and communications systems installed
- [ ] Irrigation and agricultural systems installed
- [ ] Roads, drainage, and access routes operational
- [ ] Environmental protection and enhancement works complete
- [ ] Temporary facilities removed or transferred
- [ ] Construction waste removed
- [ ] Defects and punch-list items recorded

### Handover documentation

The construction team should provide:

- As-built drawings
- Survey and underground-service records
- Equipment serial-number lists
- Inspection and test certificates
- Material certificates
- Manufacturer manuals
- Warranty documents
- Maintenance schedules
- Training records
- Environmental completion report
- Agricultural infrastructure records
- Open-defect and close-out register
- Final cost and change report

The handover package should be reviewed before commissioning begins so that missing documents do not delay grid energization or operational acceptance.

## 5.6.8 Common Construction Risks and Mitigation

| Risk | Consequence | Early indicator | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Poor ground conditions** | Foundation redesign and cost increase | Pile refusal, unexpected groundwater, weak soil | Geotechnical investigation, testing, and contingency |
| **Weather delay** | Schedule slippage and access damage | Saturated soils, storms, wind warnings | Seasonal planning, temporary roads, schedule float |
| **Unsafe work practices** | Injury, stoppage, regulatory action | Repeated near misses or incomplete inductions | Strong HSE supervision, audits, stop-work authority |
| **Equipment delivery delay** | Idle crews and delayed commissioning | Missed manufacturing or shipping milestones | Track critical items, alternative suppliers, early orders |
| **Agricultural disruption** | Yield loss or farmer conflict | Blocked routes during key farm activities | Coordinate weekly with operator and preserve access |
| **Drainage failure** | Erosion, flooding, environmental impact | Ponding or sediment leaving site | Inspect after rainfall and maintain controls |
| **Electrical installation defect** | Safety risk, rework, commissioning delay | Failed insulation or continuity tests | Hold points, competent installers, staged testing |
| **Quality defects** | Rework and warranty disputes | High defect recurrence or incomplete records | Inspection test plans and independent QA/QC |
| **Cost escalation** | Funding gap or reduced returns | Contingency rapidly consumed | Monthly forecast, change control, early decisions |
| **Community complaints** | Work restrictions or reputational damage | Repeated traffic, noise, or access complaints | Communication plan and responsive complaint handling |

---

## Conclusion

Construction and Installation is the phase in which the project's technical, agricultural, environmental, and commercial decisions become physical reality. Successful delivery depends on disciplined preparation, competent supervision, clear interfaces, and continuous coordination with the farmer, local community, authorities, grid operator, and contractors.

The most important construction principles are:

- Mobilize only when the project is genuinely construction-ready.
- Protect soil, water, biodiversity, and agricultural access from the beginning.
- Treat the PV and agricultural systems as one integrated facility.
- Use formal inspection, testing, documentation, and change-control processes.
- Maintain realistic schedule and cost contingencies.
- Record as-built information while work is visible, not after the site is complete.
- Do not move to commissioning until safety, quality, documentation, and handover requirements are satisfied.

Once mechanical completion has been achieved and the handover package is substantially complete, the project can proceed to Section 5.7: Commissioning and Testing.
