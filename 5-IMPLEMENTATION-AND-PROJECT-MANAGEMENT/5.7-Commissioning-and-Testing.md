# Section 5.7: Commissioning and Testing

## Introduction

Commissioning and Testing bridges mechanical completion and full operational readiness. This phase validates that the PV system, agricultural systems, monitoring infrastructure, and environmental protection measures all function according to design intent and can be operated safely and reliably.

Commissioning typically occurs during months 33–36 of the project timeline, though it may overlap with final construction activities. It is distinct from mechanical completion because it requires the systems to be energized, operated under realistic conditions, and measured against performance expectations.

Commissioning should be directed by qualified engineers and contractors with proven experience in grid-connected PV systems and, where relevant, agricultural integration. The test procedures should be comprehensive yet time-bound, with clear pass/fail criteria and escalation paths for defects.

> **Important:** Energization and grid connection involve hazardous voltages and must be performed only by competent personnel following French electrical standards, the grid operator's requirements, and applicable safety regulations. Do not proceed with energization without formal approval from the grid operator and completion of all required pre-energization inspections.

## 5.7.1 Pre-Commissioning Readiness

### Completion of construction activities

Before energization, all construction work that could affect electrical safety or system performance must be substantially complete:

- [ ] All PV modules installed and visually inspected
- [ ] All mounting structures confirmed structurally complete and inspected
- [ ] All electrical cabinets, enclosures, and switchgear installed and sealed
- [ ] All cables terminated, labelled, and routed according to approved design
- [ ] All underground services buried and backfilled
- [ ] All grounding and bonding connections installed and verified
- [ ] Lightning protection installed and verified
- [ ] Emergency-stop and isolation devices operational
- [ ] Monitoring and communications systems installed
- [ ] Access to inverters and electrical equipment confirmed safe and unobstructed
- [ ] Site perimeter secured and warning signs installed
- [ ] Temporary construction activities and equipment removed
- [ ] No outstanding safety punch-list items
- [ ] Environmental works and soil restoration complete
- [ ] Agricultural infrastructure operational or ready for commissioning

### Defect rectification before energization

The contractor should verify that all defects identified during construction inspections have been corrected and closed out:

- [ ] Review the defect log and confirm closure evidence for each item
- [ ] Photographic or inspection records showing defect correction
- [ ] Retesting performed where defect correction required it
- [ ] Quality-assurance sign-off documented
- [ ] No outstanding defects affecting safety or performance

No energization should proceed while safety-critical defects remain open.

### Pre-energization inspection checklist

An independent or senior-management inspection should verify:

**Electrical Safety:**
- [ ] Insulation resistance tests passed on all circuits
- [ ] No obvious electrical hazards (damaged cables, loose connections, water ingress)
- [ ] All isolation devices and emergency stops functional
- [ ] Protective-device settings reviewed and confirmed correct
- [ ] Grounding and bonding complete and verified
- [ ] No unauthorized or temporary wiring
- [ ] Cable trays and conduit intact and properly supported
- [ ] No cable damage or improper routing

**Structural and Civil:**
- [ ] Mounting structures secure and undamaged
- [ ] Access roads and walkways safe and stable
- [ ] Drainage systems clear and operational
- [ ] No obvious settlement or instability
- [ ] Temporary shoring or supports removed safely
- [ ] Roof or structural penetrations properly sealed

**Agricultural and Environmental:**
- [ ] Irrigation systems commissioned and tested
- [ ] Access for farm machinery confirmed
- [ ] Soil restoration complete and settled
- [ ] Environmental protection measures in place
- [ ] No storage of materials in restricted areas
- [ ] Vegetation and landscaping stabilized

**Documentation:**
- [ ] As-built drawings provided and reviewed
- [ ] Equipment certificates and manuals available
- [ ] Inspection records and test results organized
- [ ] Handover package substantially complete
- [ ] Operating and maintenance manuals available in accessible locations
- [ ] Training records for operations and maintenance staff

### Permit and regulatory clearance

Before energization, confirm:

- [ ] Building permits issued or sign-off obtained from relevant authority
- [ ] Electrical work approved by competent person or authority
- [ ] Environmental conditions met or monitoring plan activated
- [ ] Grid-connection application approved by DSO/TSO
- [ ] Grid-connection agreement executed
- [ ] Insurance policies active and covering commissioning and operation
- [ ] Any deferred or conditional approvals resolved

Any regulatory or permitting gaps should be escalated and resolved before energization proceeds.

### Stakeholder readiness confirmation

Notify and obtain confirmation from:

- [ ] Grid operator ready to supervise energization and testing
- [ ] Local authority aware of energization date and access
- [ ] Farmer or land occupier prepared for electrical safety measures and monitoring
- [ ] Emergency responders aware of electrical hazards on site
- [ ] Maintenance and operating personnel trained and on site
- [ ] Insurance and warranty providers aware of imminent energization

## 5.7.2 Electrical Commissioning and Testing

### Insulation and continuity testing (cold commissioning)

Before any energization, perform comprehensive electrical testing on all circuits:

**DC-side testing:**
- Insulation resistance between positive, negative, and earth on all strings and combiners
- Minimum acceptable values per IEC 61010 or project specification
- Record results on a circuit-by-circuit basis
- Investigate and remedy any weak insulation
- Continuity of all DC interconnections
- Polarity verification on all DC connections

**AC-side testing:**
- Insulation resistance on all AC circuits before transformer energization
- Three-phase AC circuits tested for phase balance and continuity
- Medium-voltage circuits tested per applicable standard
- All cables tested in service condition

**Grounding and bonding:**
- Continuity of main earthing conductor and all protective conductors
- Earth resistance of main earthing electrode
- Verification of equipotential bonding
- Lightning conductor continuity to earth

### Inverter and transformer testing

Perform manufacturer-recommended commissioning procedures for each inverter and transformer:

**Inverter testing:**
- Visual inspection for transport or storage damage
- Verification of serial number and rating
- Confirmation of firmware version and settings
- Fan operation and cooling system function
- Display and communication interface operation
- Earth-leakage and residual-current-device response
- Protective-relay settings verified against approved design
- Dummy-load testing or low-power start-up as recommended
- Grid-support and curtailment-response settings tested

**Transformer testing:**
- Oil or fluid levels and condition
- Temperature sensors and alarms operational
- No evidence of moisture or contamination
- Tap-changer operation if present
- Three-phase voltage and phase-angle relationships
- Load loss and efficiency testing per applicable standard
- Insulation resistance after drying if required

### String testing and module performance

Test PV strings before final integration:

- Visual inspection of modules for visible defects
- Open-circuit voltage (Voc) verification on each string
- String current measurement under sunlight
- Comparison against theoretical performance
- Investigation of outlier strings
- Infrared thermography to identify hot spots or cell defects
- Module I–V curve testing if equipment is available

Do not proceed with a string that shows significant performance degradation or defects that could affect safety or warranty.

### Grid interconnection testing

Before grid connection, coordinate with the grid operator to perform:

- Grid-voltage and frequency measurements
- Verification of grid-connection point and protection
- Testing of utility disconnect and anti-islanding devices
- Protective-relay coordination and testing
- Reverse-power and over-frequency response
- Voltage-rise testing and harmonic assessment
- Three-phase balance at point of connection
- Communication and control interface testing with grid operator
- Grid operator's acceptance inspection

The grid operator must formally approve the connection before energization proceeds.

### Safety device functional testing

Test all safety systems:

- Emergency-stop button operation and isolation effect
- Mechanical isolators and lockout/tagout procedures
- Automatic islanding protection
- AC and DC arc-fault detection and response
- Surge protection and coordinated operation
- Residual-current protection and sensitivity
- Thermal cutouts and temperature monitoring
- Remote disconnection or curtailment functions

Simulate a fault condition where safe and document the response.

## 5.7.3 Soft Commissioning (Initial Energization)

### Staged energization protocol

Soft commissioning uses a staged approach to bring the system to full power gradually while monitoring for unexpected behavior:

**Stage 1: DC Side Energization (no grid connection)**
- Energize the PV array on a clear, sunny day
- Confirm PV voltage and current readings
- Verify string combiner operation
- Monitor inverter input signals
- Confirm cooling fans and thermal management
- Confirm monitoring and data transmission

**Stage 2: Inverter Startup (isolated from grid)**
- Start inverter on sunny day with grid switch open
- Confirm inverter output frequency and voltage generation
- Verify islanding protection by closing and opening grid disconnect
- Confirm inverter response to irradiance changes
- Monitor for unexpected alarms or warnings
- Confirm communications and monitoring data

**Stage 3: Grid Connection (low irradiance)**
- Coordinate with grid operator to close grid disconnect
- Confirm voltage synchronization and phase locking
- Verify protective-relay response
- Monitor power injection on cloudy day or early/late day
- Confirm metering and data recording
- Monitor for grid harmonics and power quality
- Verify automatic disconnect and reconnection sequences

**Stage 4: Full-Power Operation**
- Allow system to reach full power under high irradiance
- Monitor inverter and transformer temperatures
- Confirm transformer cooling operation
- Verify load rejection and over-frequency response
- Monitor for protective-device operation
- Confirm grid operator interaction
- Extended operation at full power (hours to days)

**Stage 5: Advanced Functions**
- Test reactive-power or volt-var control if specified
- Test frequency-droop or volt-watt functions
- Test remote-curtailment or dispatch commands
- Test energy-storage integration if applicable
- Test agricultural-load dispatch if applicable

Each stage should have clear entry criteria, defined duration, specified measurements, pass/fail thresholds, and a documented handoff to the next stage.

### Monitoring and data logging

During soft commissioning, continuously log:

- PV voltage and current (string and combiner level)
- Inverter input and output power
- Inverter efficiency and losses
- Grid voltage, current, frequency, and phase angle
- Transformer temperature and oil level
- Irradiance and ambient temperature
- Weather conditions and any alarms
- All protection device activations
- Any automatic shutdown or curtailment events

Data should be timestamped and retained for analysis after the system returns to normal operation.

### Issue identification and escalation

During soft commissioning, anomalies such as the following should trigger investigation:

- Inverter shutdown or protection activation
- Unexpectedly low power output
- Voltage or frequency drifts
- Oscillations in power output
- Temperature alarms
- Monitoring or communications failures
- Protective-device nuisance trips
- Phase imbalance
- Harmonic distortion exceeding limits

Anomalies should be assigned to a responsible engineer with target closure within 24–48 hours. Options include:

1. Accept and monitor (if impact is minimal)
2. Adjust settings or control logic
3. Correct equipment or wiring defect
4. Return to previous stage until resolved
5. Escalate to manufacturer or grid operator

No anomaly should be dismissed without documented justification.

### Soft commissioning duration

Soft commissioning typically spans:

- **1–2 weeks** for staged energization and initial operation
- **2–4 weeks** for extended monitoring and issue resolution
- **1 month** for validation under varied weather and load conditions

A project should not be declared commissioned until the system has operated stably for at least 2–4 weeks without recurrence of issues identified during soft commissioning.

## 5.7.4 Agricultural System Commissioning

### Irrigation system testing and calibration

Test and calibrate the irrigation system under controlled conditions:

- **Flow and pressure testing:** Measure flow rate and pressure at representative outlets and compare against design.
- **Uniformity testing:** Operate the system and observe water distribution across the field. Identify and correct plugged drippers or uneven pressure zones.
- **Automation testing:** Verify that soil-moisture sensors, weather stations, timers, and controllers operate correctly and respond to setpoints.
- **Pump performance:** Confirm pump output matches design and that throttling, frequency drive, or pressure-relief settings are correct.
- **Leak detection:** Inspect for leaks at connections, fittings, and valves; correct any identified leaks.
- **Flushing and filtration:** Flush lines to remove sediment and debris; confirm that filters are clean and functioning.
- **Winterization or draining procedures:** Verify that the operator understands procedures to protect the system in winter if required.

Document the calibrated setpoints and baseline performance for reference during operations.

### Crop establishment and growth conditions

Confirm that the agricultural environment is suitable for the intended crops:

- **Light environment:** Measure light levels under the panels at different locations and compare against requirements for the selected crops. Document any adaptation needs.
- **Soil conditions:** Confirm that soil pH, nutrient content, and moisture-retention capability are suitable for planting. Make any final amendments.
- **Access and working clearance:** Verify with the farmer that machinery can access all planting and harvest areas without interference.
- **Pest and disease risk:** Assess whether the microclimate creates conditions favorable to problematic pests or diseases; confirm that mitigation measures are in place.
- **Livestock safety:** If animals are present, verify that fencing, gates, water access, and shelter are appropriate and safe.

### Crop planting and establishment

Work with the farmer to plan the crop-planting calendar:

- Coordinate planting dates with panel installation completion and final surface restoration.
- Procure appropriate seed or planting stock.
- Establish the initial crop area and density.
- Document initial plant establishment success or any replanting required.
- Plan nutrient and pest-management programs for the growing season.
- Establish baseline yield and growth benchmarks for comparison in subsequent seasons.

### Environmental enhancement features

If environmental features such as hedgerows, native plantings, or biodiversity structures were constructed, confirm:

- Planted species are established and surviving
- Fencing or exclusion measures are effective
- Pollinator or wildlife habitat conditions are suitable
- Wetland or pond areas are holding water and supporting vegetation
- Monitoring equipment (cameras, water-level sensors, etc.) is operational

Schedule follow-up surveys or assessments to verify establishment after one growing season.

## 5.7.5 Monitoring System Commissioning

### Sensor installation and calibration

Verify that all monitoring sensors are installed correctly, calibrated, and collecting data:

**PV system monitoring:**
- Irradiance sensors: calibration certificate verified and sensor orientation correct
- Temperature sensors: accurate placement and good thermal contact
- Power meters: polarity and phase sequence correct; accuracy verified
- Data-acquisition modules: correct wiring and addressed correctly on network

**Agricultural and environmental monitoring:**
- Soil-moisture sensors: installed at representative depths and calibrated for soil type
- Rainfall gauges: properly exposed and draining correctly
- Wind speed and direction sensors: at representative height with no obstructions
- Water-level sensors: installed in wells, ponds, or watercourses; calibrated to known datum
- Irrigation flow meters: installed in line and readable
- Water quality probes: calibrated and verified before and after commissioning

Create a sensor-calibration matrix that documents the as-installed condition and baseline measurements.

### Data-transmission testing

Test end-to-end data flow from sensors to storage and display:

- Confirm that all sensors are transmitting data continuously
- Verify that data is received and stored correctly
- Check data quality for missing values, outliers, or anomalies
- Test backup power or redundant communications
- Verify cybersecurity measures (encryption, authentication, network isolation)
- Confirm that alarms are triggered and routed correctly
- Test data visualization and user interface
- Verify data export and backup procedures

Test the system under varied conditions: clear sky, cloud cover, rain, high and low temperatures, and different network loads.

### User interface and alerting

Train the operator and manager on:

- Access and login procedures
- Navigating performance dashboards
- Viewing real-time and historical data
- Setting and modifying alerts
- Interpreting alarms and warnings
- Exporting reports
- Troubleshooting communication issues
- Contacting support if problems arise

Create quick-reference guides and recorded demonstrations if helpful.

### Baseline establishment

Once the monitoring system is stable and validated, establish baseline metrics:

- Average daily energy yield under various weather conditions
- Inverter efficiency, losses, and downtime
- Grid-export power and revenue
- Agricultural yield, water use, and inputs
- Soil moisture and irrigation response
- Rainfall and ambient temperatures
- Species presence or biodiversity indices

Baselines enable early detection of performance changes or problems during operations.

## 5.7.6 Performance Testing and Validation

### Measured versus predicted performance

Compare actual energy production against pre-construction predictions:

- **Irradiance:** Measure on-site irradiance and compare against historical averages or nearby weather stations.
- **Module performance:** Calculate expected DC power from irradiance and compare with measured PV output.
- **Inverter efficiency:** Measure DC input and AC output power; calculate and compare against manufacturer specifications.
- **System losses:** Estimate conduction, transformation, and metering losses; compare against design assumptions.
- **Performance ratio:** Calculate actual energy yield divided by theoretical yield; typical targets are 75–85% for ground-mounted systems in France.

If measured performance is significantly below predictions, investigate:

- Underperforming strings or modules (thermal, soiling, wiring issues)
- Inverter operating below rated efficiency (temperature, loading)
- Grid curtailment or unavailability
- Monitoring or metering errors
- Atmospheric conditions differing from historical data

Document the findings and adjust long-term performance expectations if warranted.

### Efficiency and loss analysis

Perform a detailed loss analysis by measuring power at each stage:

**Typical loss breakdown:**

| Loss Category | Typical Range |
|---|---|
| Spectral and soiling | 2–5% |
| Temperature | 3–8% |
| Mismatch and wiring | 2–3% |
| Inverter efficiency | 2–5% |
| Transformer efficiency | 1–2% |
| Metering and balance-of-system | 1–2% |
| **Total system loss** | **12–25%** |
| **Performance Ratio** | **75–88%** |

If actual losses exceed expected ranges, identify the cause and determine whether correction is warranted.

### Weather-dependent validation

Over several weeks of commissioning, observe performance under varying weather:

- **Clear sky days:** Peak power output; efficiency and temperature rise
- **Overcast days:** Part-load efficiency; stable temperatures
- **Rainy days:** Soiling effects before and after rain; minimal output
- **Cold days:** Enhanced efficiency at low temperatures; performance gains
- **Hot days:** Temperature derate; efficiency losses

This weather diversity provides confidence that the system performs as expected across typical seasonal conditions.

### Agricultural production validation

For agrivoltaic projects, observe early agricultural production:

- **Crop growth:** Monitor plant height, leaf area, and biomass development over the growing season.
- **Crop yield:** After harvest, measure yield and compare against farmer's historical yields on unshaded land or similar farms.
- **Yield shade reduction:** If yield is reduced under panels compared to open-field baseline, quantify the reduction and verify it is within expectations (typically 10–30% depending on crop and configuration).
- **Quality and market attributes:** Confirm that crop quality, marketability, and prices are acceptable.
- **Labor and input requirements:** Track actual labor hours and input costs and compare against budget.

Document findings for use in optimizing management practices and validating the business model.

## 5.7.7 Operator Training and Handover

### Operations staff training

Before handover, ensure operations personnel are trained and competent in:

**Day-to-day operation:**
- Daily visual inspection checklist
- Monitoring system navigation and alarm response
- Response procedures for common faults or alarms
- Manual override and emergency procedures
- Hazard awareness and electrical safety
- Livestock management if animals are present
- Irrigation operation and manual overrides

**Maintenance fundamentals:**
- Module and structure cleaning procedures
- Inverter and transformer inspections
- Cable and connection visual checks
- Monitoring equipment maintenance
- Soiling assessment and response
- Pest or disease monitoring
- Record-keeping requirements

**Reporting and documentation:**
- Energy production recording
- Agricultural production tracking
- Maintenance logs and spare-parts management
- Incident and near-miss reporting
- Contact lists for fault response and escalation
- Annual inspection and service scheduling

### Training delivery

Training should include:

- **Classroom or didactic sessions:** Covering system overview, hazards, operational procedures, and troubleshooting.
- **On-site demonstrations:** Walking through procedures with actual equipment, showing displays, and practicing alarm response.
- **Supervised operation:** Allowing staff to operate the system under supervision during the first weeks of full operation.
- **Written materials:** Manuals, quick-reference guides, and checklists left on site.
- **Recorded video:** Demonstrations and tutorials accessible to refresher training.
- **Contact details:** Support numbers for the contractor, equipment suppliers, and grid operator.

Confirm that trained personnel pass a practical assessment before being signed off as competent.

### Maintenance and service plan

Establish a maintenance plan that covers:

- **Daily checks:** Visual inspection checklist
- **Monthly checks:** Inverter and monitoring system review
- **Quarterly checks:** Cable and connection inspection; irrigation system review
- **Annual service:** Professional cleaning, electrical testing, and equipment servicing
- **Seasonal preparations:** Winterization, drain-down, or other seasonal measures
- **Warranty periods and coverage:** Identify equipment under warranty and requirements for claiming coverage

Schedule services in advance and maintain records.

### Handover documentation

Deliver a comprehensive handover package including:

- **System overview document:** Description, ratings, and key operating parameters
- **As-built drawings:** PV layout, electrical single-line diagram, agricultural infrastructure, utilities, and drainage
- **Operating manual:** Step-by-step procedures for start-up, operation, shutdown, and emergency response
- **Maintenance manual:** Inspection checklists, preventive-maintenance schedules, and troubleshooting guides
- **Equipment manuals:** Inverter, transformer, monitoring system, irrigation controller, and other key equipment
- **Commissioning reports:** Testing results, baseline performance, and as-found conditions
- **Warranty and insurance documents:** Proof of coverage and claims procedures
- **Spare-parts list:** Common consumables and recommended spares to keep on hand
- **Contacts:** Support and emergency contacts for the contractor, suppliers, grid operator, and local authority
- **Performance baseline:** Typical energy yield, efficiency, and agricultural production under representative conditions

Organize documentation in a physical folder or cloud repository accessible to operations and maintenance staff.

## 5.7.8 Post-Commissioning Monitoring and Optimization

### Performance monitoring during first operating season

During the first growing season, intensive monitoring should detect and address issues early:

- **Energy production:** Track daily and monthly energy yield and compare against baseline and predictions.
- **System availability:** Monitor downtime, protection activations, and fault frequency.
- **Environmental conditions:** Verify that irradiance, temperature, and weather align with historical data.
- **Agricultural production:** Monitor crop growth, water use, and interim productivity indicators.
- **Monitoring data quality:** Confirm that all sensors are functioning and data is complete.

Establish a cadence for reviewing performance: weekly for the first month, then bi-weekly or monthly as confidence increases.

### Fault response and defect resolution

Develop a process for responding to faults or performance anomalies:

1. **Detection:** Automated alarms or operator observation
2. **Classification:** Distinguish between operational issues, maintenance defects, and design problems
3. **Investigation:** Gather data, inspect equipment, and determine root cause
4. **Corrective action:** Repair, adjustment, setting change, or design modification
5. **Verification:** Confirm that the issue is resolved and performance has recovered
6. **Documentation:** Record the fault, action, and outcome for future reference

Establish an escalation path for issues that cannot be resolved by operations staff.

### Soiling assessment and cleaning

Monitor panel soiling and implement a cleaning schedule:

- **Monthly inspection:** Visual assessment of module cleanliness
- **Performance monitoring:** Track any drop in performance that might indicate soiling
- **Rain response:** Observe whether natural rainfall maintains acceptable cleanliness
- **Cleaning trigger:** Establish a cleaning threshold (e.g., 5–10% performance loss) that triggers intervention
- **Cleaning method:** Dry brushing or water spray (no chemicals or abrasive methods)
- **Record keeping:** Log cleaning dates and observed soiling levels

Soiling requirements vary by region and climate; adjust the strategy based on first-year experience.

### Seasonal optimization

As the first season progresses, optimize for the next:

- **Agricultural management:** Adjust crop spacing, variety, irrigation timing, or pest management based on observed performance
- **Inverter settings:** Refine reactive-power, volt-var, or frequency-droop settings based on observed grid conditions
- **Monitoring thresholds:** Adjust alarm setpoints based on observed normal ranges
- **Maintenance schedule:** Adjust the frequency of inspections or services based on actual wear or degradation rates
- **Staff procedures:** Document lessons learned and update training materials

## 5.7.9 Common Commissioning Issues and Solutions

| Issue | Symptom | Investigation | Solution |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Low DC voltage** | String voltage below spec | Check for cloudy conditions, soiling, or module defects | Clean modules; test underperforming module; verify irradiance |
| **String current imbalance** | One string significantly below others | Shading, loose connection, or failed diode | Eliminate shading; check connectors and bypass diodes |
| **Inverter shutdown at startup** | Inverter fault on DC energization | Leakage current, residual charge, or protection setting | Check for contamination; allow charge bleed-off; verify settings |
| **Grid connection rejection** | Utility cannot close switch or protection trips | Voltage/frequency mismatch or harmonic distortion | Confirm synchronization; measure grid voltage/frequency; check harmonics |
| **Low AC power output** | Consistently below 80% of DC input | High inverter temperature, low grid voltage, or efficiency issue | Check cooling; measure grid voltage and phase; verify efficiency curve |
| **Erratic monitoring data** | Spikes, gaps, or impossible values | Sensor failure, loose connection, or data-transmission error | Check sensor connections; recalibrate; verify data link |
| **Inverter temperature alarm** | Frequent high-temperature shutdown | Hot weather, high power output, or fan failure | Check ambient temperature; confirm cooling airflow; test fan |
| **Crop underperformance** | Yield or growth significantly below expectations | Inadequate light, water, or nutrient; pests or disease | Measure light under panels; assess irrigation; scout for pests |
| **Irrigation malfunction** | Low pressure or uneven delivery | Clogged filter, leak, or pump issue | Clean or replace filter; check for leaks; test pump discharge |
| **Monitoring communications failure** | No data received at server | Network interruption or modem/router issue | Check network connectivity; restart equipment; verify firewall rules |

## 5.7.10 Handover Sign-Off and Transition to Operations

### Final handover checklist

Before signing off commissioning, confirm:

- [ ] All pre-commissioning defects resolved
- [ ] Electrical testing and certification complete
- [ ] Soft commissioning and extended monitoring complete
- [ ] Performance aligned with design or documented variance explained
- [ ] Agricultural system producing or demonstrating capability
- [ ] Monitoring system operational and baseline established
- [ ] Training completed and competency verified
- [ ] All documentation delivered and organized
- [ ] Warranties and insurance policies active
- [ ] Operations staff and procedures ready
- [ ] Grid operator approval and connection agreement active
- [ ] No outstanding safety or regulatory issues

A formal sign-off by the project manager, contractor, operator, and grid operator (where applicable) should be documented.

### Transition to routine operations

Once commissioning is formally complete, the system transitions to routine operations:

- Day-to-day monitoring and maintenance by operations staff
- Performance tracking and reporting
- Seasonal optimization and adjustments
- Planned maintenance and service
- Annual inspections and certifications
- Ongoing monitoring of regulatory compliance

The contractor's role shifts from active operation to support and advisory, available for complex troubleshooting or warranty claims.

### First-year performance review

After 12 months of operation, conduct a comprehensive review:

- **Energy production:** Compare annual yield against predictions and identify causes of variance
- **Agricultural production:** Evaluate crop yields, quality, and market acceptance
- **Availability and reliability:** Review downtime, fault frequency, and maintenance requirements
- **Monitoring data:** Confirm that baseline metrics are stable and sensor accuracy is maintained
- **Financial performance:** Review actual revenue against budgeted revenue
- **Environmental performance:** Assess biodiversity, soil health, water use, and compliance with environmental commitments
- **Stakeholder satisfaction:** Gather feedback from the farmer, community, grid operator, and investors
- **Optimization opportunities:** Identify improvements for the second and subsequent years

Document findings and implement changes to improve future performance.

---

## Conclusion

Commissioning and Testing transforms the completed construction into a reliably operating facility. Success depends on:

- **Preparation:** Ensuring that mechanical completion has achieved the required quality and safety standards
- **Staged approach:** Bringing systems online gradually while monitoring for unexpected behavior
- **Comprehensive testing:** Validating electrical, agricultural, monitoring, and environmental systems
- **Operator competency:** Training staff thoroughly and verifying their ability to operate and respond to issues
- **Documentation:** Creating clear records of as-found conditions, baseline performance, and operating procedures
- **Continuous improvement:** Monitoring performance during the first season and optimizing for subsequent years

Once commissioning is complete and formal handover accepted, the project transitions to Section 5.8: Operations and Maintenance, which covers the ongoing 20–25 year operating life of the facility.
