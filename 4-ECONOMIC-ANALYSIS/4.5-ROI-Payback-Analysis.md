# 4.5 ROI AND PAYBACK ANALYSIS

## Overview
This section provides detailed analysis of return on investment (ROI), payback periods, and comparative financial performance for agrivoltaic projects. It includes case studies, benchmarking against alternative investments, and guidance on evaluating project viability.

---

## 4.5.1 ROI Calculation Methods

### Simple Return on Investment (ROI)

**Definition**: Profit earned divided by initial investment, expressed as percentage

**Formula**:
```
Simple ROI (%) = [Net Profit / Initial Investment] × 100

Example:
Net Profit (Year 1): €391,000
Initial Investment: €1,780,000
Simple ROI = (€391,000 / €1,780,000) × 100 = 21.9%
```

**Interpretation**:
- 21.9% ROI means the project returns 21.9% of invested capital annually
- High single-year ROI due to favorable solar FIT and agricultural revenue mix
- Useful for quick feasibility screening but limited for long-term analysis

---

### Annualized Return on Investment (ARoI)

**Definition**: Average annual return calculated over entire project lifetime

**Formula**:
```
ARoI (%) = [Cumulative Net Cash Flow / (Initial Investment × Project Years)] × 100

Example (25-year project):
Cumulative Net Profit (25 years): €9,750,000
Initial Investment: €1,780,000
Project Years: 25
ARoI = (€9,750,000 / (€1,780,000 × 25)) × 100 = 21.9%
```

**Interpretation**:
- Accounts for long-term cash flow sustainability
- Better for comparing projects with different lifespans
- Assumes reinvestment at project average return

---

### Cash-on-Cash Return

**Definition**: Annual cash flow as percentage of actual cash invested (equity)

**Formula**:
```
Cash-on-Cash Return = [Annual Net Cash Flow / Equity Investment] × 100

Example:
Annual Net Cash Flow: €391,000
Equity Investment (after subsidy + debt): €468,000 (net)
Cash-on-Cash = (€391,000 / €468,000) × 100 = 83.5%
```

**Interpretation**:
- 83.5% means €1 of equity generates €0.835 annual cash return
- Excellent for equity investors (typically seek 15-25% cash-on-cash)
- Particularly strong when debt financing used (leverage amplifies returns)

---

### Equity Multiple (Money Multiple)

**Definition**: Total cash returned divided by initial equity investment

**Formula**:
```
Equity Multiple = Cumulative Cash Returned / Initial Equity Investment

Example (25-year project):
Cumulative Cash Returned: €9,750,000
Initial Equity Investment: €468,000
Equity Multiple = €9,750,000 / €468,000 = 20.8x
```

**Interpretation**:
- 20.8x means investor's equity grows to 20.8 times original amount
- Equivalent to compound annual return ~18%
- Professional investors typically seek 2-5x equity multiple over 10-25 years
- This project significantly exceeds institutional standards

## 4.5.2 Payback Period Analysis

### Simple Payback Period

**Definition**: Time required to recover initial investment from cumulative cash flows

**Formula**:
```
Simple Payback = Initial Investment / Average Annual Cash Flow

Example:
Initial Investment: €1,780,000
Average Annual Cash Flow: €391,000
Simple Payback = €1,780,000 / €391,000 = 4.5 years
```

**Limitations**:
- Doesn't account for time value of money
- Ignores cash flows after payback
- Can underestimate risk for volatile projects

**French Agrivoltaic Typical Range**: 5-12 years (higher end if agriculture underperforms)

---

### Discounted Payback Period

**Definition**: Time to payback adjusted for time value of money using discount rate

**Calculation Method**:
1. Discount each year's cash flow using 6% discount rate
2. Accumulate discounted cash flows
3. Find year when cumulative discounted cash flow ≥ initial investment

**Example (1.5 MW Project)**:

| Year | Nominal Cash Flow | Discount Factor (6%) | Discounted Cash Flow | Cumulative |
|------|------------------|---------------------|----------------------|-----------|
| 0 | -€1,780,000 | 1.000 | -€1,780,000 | -€1,780,000 |
| 1 | €391,000 | 0.943 | €368,813 | -€1,411,187 |
| 2 | €392,000 | 0.890 | €348,880 | -€1,062,307 |
| 3 | €393,500 | 0.840 | €330,360 | -€731,947 |
| 4 | €395,000 | 0.792 | €313,080 | -€418,867 |
| 5 | €396,500 | 0.747 | €296,230 | -€122,637 |
| 6 | €398,000 | 0.705 | €280,590 | €157,953 |

**Discounted Payback Period**: 5.4 years (between year 5 and 6)

**Interpretation**:
- Project recovers inflation-adjusted investment in 5.4 years
- More realistic than simple payback
- Still conservative (ignores time value after payback)

---

### Payback Period by System Size

**Comparison: How system size affects payback**

| System Size | Annual Cash Flow | Payback (Simple) | Payback (Discounted, 6%) |
|------------|------------------|------------------|--------------------------|
| **500 kW** | €180,000 | 3.3 years | 3.8 years |
| **1 MW** | €310,000 | 4.8 years | 5.5 years |
| **1.5 MW** | €391,000 | 4.5 years | 5.4 years |
| **3 MW** | €700,000 | 3.9 years | 4.5 years |

**Pattern**: Larger systems have faster payback (economies of scale)

### Probability-Weighted Returns

**Expected Return Analysis** (assigning probabilities to scenarios):

Assuming:
- Conservative scenario: 20% probability (poor conditions)
- Base scenario: 50% probability (most likely)
- Optimistic scenario: 30% probability (favorable conditions)

**Probability-Weighted Metrics**:

```
Expected IRR = (0.20 × 6.8%) + (0.50 × 13.2%) + (0.30 × 18.1%)
             = 1.36% + 6.60% + 5.43%
             = 13.4%

Expected 25-Year NPV = (0.20 × €0.45M) + (0.50 × €1.85M) + (0.30 × €3.20M)
                     = €0.09M + €0.925M + €0.96M

Expected Payback = (0.20 × 9.2) + (0.50 × 4.8) + (0.30 × 2.6)
                 = 1.84 + 2.4 + 0.78

```

**Interpretation**:
- Probability-weighted IRR (13.4%) slightly exceeds base case (13.2%)
- Risk-adjusted returns remain attractive
- Downside protection: Even conservative scenario generates 6.8% IRR (viable)

## 4.5.6 Break-Even Analysis

### Minimum Conditions for Project Viability

**What assumptions must hold for project to achieve 8% IRR (acceptable minimum)?**

**Single-Variable Break-Even Analysis**:

#### Minimum FIT Rate Required

```
At what feed-in tariff does IRR = 8%?

Sensitivity: For every €0.01/kWh decrease in FIT rate, IRR drops ~0.8%

If base FIT = €0.17/kWh and base IRR = 13.2%:
Target IRR: 8%
Required decrease: (13.2% - 8%) / 0.8% per €0.01 = 6.5 × €0.01 = €0.065/kWh decrease
Minimum FIT required: €0.17 - €0.065 = €0.105/kWh
```

**Interpretation**: Project remains viable with FIT as low as €0.105/kWh (well above expected floor of €0.10/kWh)

---

#### Minimum Agricultural Revenue Required

```
At what revenue level does IRR = 8%?

Sensitivity: For every 10% reduction in ag revenue, IRR drops ~2%

If base ag revenue = €415,000 and base IRR = 13.2%:
Required decrease: (13.2% - 8%) / 2% = 2.6 × 10% = 26% reduction
Minimum ag revenue: €415,000 × (1 - 0.26) = €306,000 (-€109,000)
```

**Interpretation**: Agricultural revenue can drop by 26% and project still hits 8% IRR threshold

---

#### Maximum CapEx Acceptable

```
At what cost does IRR = 8%?

If base CapEx = €1.78M and base IRR = 13.2%:
Sensitivity: For every €100k increase in CapEx, IRR drops ~0.5%

Required increase to hit 8% IRR: (13.2% - 8%) / 0.5% = 10.4 × €100k = €1.04M increase
Maximum acceptable CapEx: €1.78M + €1.04M = €2.82M
```

**Interpretation**: Project can absorb cost increases up to ~60% and still achieve 8% IRR

---

### Multi-Variable Break-Even Scenarios

**Extreme Stress Test**: What if multiple assumptions deteriorate simultaneously?

**Scenario**: FIT -20%, Ag Revenue -20%, CapEx +20%

```
FIT impact: -20% of €0.17 = -€0.034/kWh loss in revenue (~-2.7% IRR)
Ag revenue impact: -20% × €415k = -€83k loss (~-2.0% IRR)
CapEx impact: +20% of €1.78M = cost increase (~-3.0% IRR)

Cumulative IRR impact: -2.7% - 2.0% - 3.0% = -7.7%
Resulting IRR: 13.2% - 7.7% = 5.5%
```

**Verdict**: Project would be marginally viable (5.5% IRR > zero, but below 8% threshold)

**Recovery Options**:
- Aggressive subsidy pursuit (+3-4% IRR)
- Operational efficiency (reduce OpEx by 10%, +1-2% IRR)
- Enhanced agricultural prices (+2-3% IRR through value-added)

### Downside Protection Analysis

**What protects the project if things go wrong?**

1. **Fixed Solar Revenue** (FIT contract):
   - Locked at €0.165-0.17/kWh for 20 years
   - Represents 42% of expected revenue (stable base)
   - Weather-resistant (panels perform even on cloudy days)

2. **Diversified Agricultural Revenue**:
   - Multiple crops/enterprises reduce single-product risk
   - Shade-tolerant vegetables have lower weather volatility
   - Livestock provides countercyclical revenue (commodities vs. electricity)

3. **Long Asset Life**:
   - Solar panels: 25-30 year warranty (lifespan extends beyond financing)
   - Infrastructure: Payback in 5-6 years; remainder is pure profit
   - Trees: 20+ year productive life (provides base income)

4. **Conservative OpEx Assumptions**:
   - Budget includes 10-15% contingency
   - Well-proven technology and practices
   - Low cost escalation risk (agricultural labor is local)

## 4.5.11 Investment Decision Framework

### When to Proceed with Agrivoltaic Investment?

**Green Light (Proceed):**
- ✅ Base case IRR > 10% (even without subsidies)
- ✅ Payback < 8 years (discounted)
- ✅ NPV > €1M (over 25 years, 6% discount rate)
- ✅ Conservative case still achieves > 6% IRR
- ✅ Strong subsidy prospects (>25% CapEx grant likely)
- ✅ Experienced farm team with growth mindset
- ✅ Secure land tenure (ownership or 25+ year lease)
- ✅ Proximity to markets (agricultural + grid)

**Yellow Light (Proceed with Caution):**
- ⚠️ IRR 8-10% (requires optimistic ag assumptions)
- ⚠️ Payback 8-12 years
- ⚠️ NPV €500K-1M
- ⚠️ Uncertain subsidy prospects
- ⚠️ Agricultural market untested
- ⚠️ First-time solar operator

**Red Light (Reconsider or Redesign):**
- ❌ Base case IRR < 8%
- ❌ Payback > 12 years (discounted)
- ❌ NPV < €500K
- ❌ Conservative case IRR < 5%
- ❌ No subsidy prospects
- ❌ Short-term land lease (<15 years)
- ❌ Inadequate farm management capability
