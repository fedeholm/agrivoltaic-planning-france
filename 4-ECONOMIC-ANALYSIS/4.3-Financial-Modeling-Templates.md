# 4.3 FINANCIAL MODELING TEMPLATES AND TOOLS

## Overview
This section provides practical financial modeling templates, software tools, and step-by-step methodologies for building comprehensive financial models for agrivoltaic projects. Templates are designed for French agrivoltaic systems and can be customized for specific project parameters.

---

## 4.3.1 Financial Modeling Fundamentals

### Key Financial Metrics for Agrivoltaic Projects

#### Net Present Value (NPV)

**Definition**: The sum of all cash flows (both costs and revenues) discounted to present value.

**Formula**:
```
NPV = Σ [CFt / (1 + r)^t] - Initial Investment

Where:
CFt = Cash flow in year t
r = Discount rate (typically 5-8% for French agrivoltaic projects)
t = Year (0 to project lifetime, typically 25-30 years)
```

**Interpretation**:
- NPV > 0: Project is financially viable (value created)
- NPV < 0: Project destroys value; not recommended
- Higher NPV = Better financial outcome

**Typical French Agrivoltaic Project**:
- NPV range: €500,000-2,000,000 (for 1-3 MW systems)
- Discount rate: 6-7% (reflects risk and cost of capital)

---

#### Internal Rate of Return (IRR)

**Definition**: The discount rate at which NPV = 0 (breakeven return rate).

**Interpretation**:
- IRR > Cost of capital (interest rate): Project is viable
- IRR > Opportunity cost: Project outperforms alternatives
- Higher IRR = Better investment

**Typical French Agrivoltaic Projects**:
- IRR range: 8-15% for well-designed systems
- Compares favorably to:
  - Bank savings: 1-2%
  - Bonds: 3-4%
  - Stock market average: 7-10%
  - Agricultural returns alone: 2-5%
  - Solar alone (without agriculture): 6-10%

---

#### Payback Period

**Definition**: Time required for cumulative cash flow to recover initial investment.

**Formula**:
```
Payback Period = Initial Investment / Average Annual Cash Flow (years)
```

**Advantages**:
- Simple to calculate and understand
- Indicates project risk (shorter = lower risk)
- Useful for quick feasibility screening

**Limitations**:
- Doesn't account for time value of money (use Discounted Payback Period for precision)
- Ignores cash flows after payback

**Typical French Agrivoltaic Projects**:
- Simple payback: 5-12 years
- Discounted payback (6% discount rate): 6-15 years
- With subsidies (35% CapEx reduction): 3-8 years

---

#### Debt Service Coverage Ratio (DSCR)

**Definition**: Annual net cash flow divided by annual debt service (principal + interest).

**Formula**:
```
DSCR = Annual Net Cash Flow / Annual Debt Payment

Minimum acceptable DSCR: 1.25 (banks typically require)
```

**Interpretation**:
- DSCR < 1.0: Insufficient cash to cover debt payments (risky)
- DSCR 1.0-1.25: Marginal; banks may require additional collateral
- DSCR > 1.25: Good coverage; loan approval likely
- DSCR > 1.5: Excellent; strong financial position

**Agrivoltaic Example**:
- Annual net cash flow: €391,000 (from 1.5 MW integrated system)
- Annual loan payment (€1.5M @ 4% over 15 years): €111,000
- DSCR = €391,000 / €111,000 = 3.52 (excellent)

---

#### Profitability Index (PI)

**Definition**: Present value of future cash flows divided by initial investment.

**Formula**:
```
PI = PV of Future Cash Flows / Initial Investment

PI > 1.0 = Project creates value
PI = 1.0 = Breakeven
PI < 1.0 = Project destroys value
```

**Use Case**: Comparing projects of different sizes; allocating limited capital

---

### Discount Rate Selection

**Factors Influencing Discount Rate**:
1. Cost of debt (interest rate on bank loan): 2.5-5.5%
2. Cost of equity (required return on owner capital): 7-12%
3. Project risk premium: 0-3%
4. Inflation expectations: 1.5-2.5%

**Recommended Discount Rate by Project Type** (France):

| Project Type | Risk Level | Typical Discount Rate |
|-------------|-----------|----------------------|
| Solar FIT (utility-scale, proven technology) | Very Low | 4-5% |
| Agrivoltaic with FIT + established agriculture | Low | 5-6% |
| Agrivoltaic mixed enterprise (new business model) | Medium | 6-8% |
| Agrivoltaic with experimental crops | Medium-High | 7-10% |
| Innovation/R&D agrivoltaic systems | High | 10-15% |

## 4.3.2 Excel-Based Financial Model Template

### Structure Overview

A comprehensive financial model typically contains these worksheets:

1. **Assumptions Sheet**: All input parameters (costs, revenues, rates)
2. **Cash Flow Statement**: Year-by-year cash inflows and outflows
3. **Income Statement**: Revenue, costs, and profit/loss
4. **Balance Sheet**: Assets, liabilities, equity
5. **Debt Schedule**: Loan repayment details
6. **Financial Metrics**: NPV, IRR, payback, DSCR calculations
7. **Sensitivity Analysis**: Impact of key assumptions changing

---

## 4.3.3 Sensitivity Analysis

### What Changes Impact Project Viability Most?

**One-Variable Sensitivity Table** (1.5 MW model):

**Impact of Feed-in Tariff Change**:

| FIT Rate | Year 1 NPV | IRR | Payback |
|----------|-----------|-----|---------|
| €0.12/kWh | €800,000 | 10.2% | 8.5 years |
| €0.15/kWh | €1,050,000 | 12.1% | 6.8 years |
| €0.17/kWh | €1,200,000 | 13.2% | 5.9 years |
| €0.20/kWh | €1,450,000 | 15.1% | 4.8 years |
| €0.25/kWh | €1,850,000 | 18.2% | 3.5 years |

**Interpretation**: Project IRR is highly sensitive to FIT rate; 0.05€/kWh change = ~2-3% IRR impact

---

**Impact of Agricultural Revenue Change**:

| Ag Revenue | Total Year 1 Revenue | Year 1 NPV | IRR | Payback |
|-----------|---------------------|-----------|-----|---------|
| -50% (crop failure) | €339,000 | €400,000 | 6.1% | 11.2 years |
| -25% (poor market) | €409,500 | €700,000 | 8.9% | 8.9 years |
| Baseline (100%) | €480,500 | €1,200,000 | 13.2% | 5.9 years |
| +25% (good market) | €551,500 | €1,600,000 | 15.8% | 4.2 years |
| +50% (excellent market) | €622,000 | €2,000,000 | 18.1% | 3.0 years |

**Interpretation**: Project remains viable even with 50% agricultural revenue loss (IRR 6.1%, though less attractive)

---

**Impact of CapEx Cost Changes**:

| Total CapEx | Annual Payment (loan) | Year 1 NPV | IRR | Payback |
|-------------|---------------------|-----------|-----|---------|
| -30% (€1,246,000) | €88,000 | €1,550,000 | 16.2% | 4.1 years |
| -15% (€1,513,000) | €107,000 | €1,375,000 | 14.6% | 4.9 years |
| Base (€1,780,000) | €126,000 | €1,200,000 | 13.2% | 5.9 years |
| +15% (€2,047,000) | €145,000 | €1,025,000 | 11.9% | 7.0 years |
| +30% (€2,314,000) | €164,000 | €850,000 | 10.6% | 8.2 years |

**Interpretation**: CapEx cost is critical; every €100k increase reduces NPV by ~€175k, reduces IRR by ~0.7%

## 4.3.4 Software Tools for Financial Modeling

### Spreadsheet-Based Tools (Most Accessible)

#### Microsoft Excel or Google Sheets (Recommended for most users)

**Advantages**:
- Familiar to most users
- Flexible and customizable
- Works offline
- No subscription required (Excel one-time purchase; Sheets free)
- Easy to share and collaborate

**Disadvantages**:
- Manual error risk (formula mistakes)
- Limited to small-scale projects without complexity
- Requires financial modeling expertise

**Setup Time**: 20-40 hours for comprehensive model

**Cost**: €69-160 (Excel) or free (Google Sheets)

---

### Specialized Solar/Agricultural Financial Tools

#### NREL's PVWatts (https://pvwatts.nrel.gov/)

**Purpose**: Solar PV system generation estimation

**Features**:
- Hourly to annual generation projection
- Weather data by location (worldwide)
- Temperature-adjusted efficiency
- System loss accounting
- Export to Excel

**Cost**: Free

**Accuracy**: ±15% typical for European systems

**Agrivoltaic Application**: Quick generation baseline estimation

---

#### PVGIS (https://pvgis.geo.esa.int/)

**Purpose**: Photovoltaic Geographical Information System

**Features**:
- High-resolution irradiance maps (1 km grid)
- 20-30 year historical data
- System configuration modeling (tilt, azimuth, losses)
- Monthly and annual projections
- Shade impact modeling (custom near horizons)

**Cost**: Free

**Accuracy**: ±5-10% for generation estimates

**Agrivoltaic Application**: Critical for elevated/angled systems (shade effects)

---

#### Helioscope (https://www.helioscope.com/)

**Purpose**: Professional solar design and financial modeling

**Features**:
- String-level design with shade analysis
- Bill of materials generation
- Customer savings calculator
- Integrated financial modeling
- Drone imagery integration

**Cost**: €50-200/month (subscription)

**Best For**: Complex projects; professional presentations

**Agrivoltaic Application**: String-level modeling for angled/elevated systems; custom shade analysis

---

#### PVsyst (https://www.pvsyst.com/)

**Purpose**: Comprehensive solar system design and simulation

**Features**:
- Detailed system modeling (panels, inverters, wiring losses)
- Shading analysis (detailed geometry)
- Performance simulation (hourly to annual)
- Financial calculations (yield, revenue, IRR)
- Report generation

**Cost**: €800-1,200 (one-time license) or €100-200/month (cloud)

**Best For**: Detailed technical analysis; complex configurations

**Agrivoltaic Application**: Precise shade modeling; multi-configuration comparison

---

#### SAM (System Advisor Model) - NREL (https://sam.nrel.gov/)

**Purpose**: Renewable energy financial modeling

**Features**:
- Solar PV, wind, geothermal, biomass modules
- Detailed financial modeling (cash flow, loan, incentives)
- Technology performance models
- Regional incentive databases
- Parametric analysis

**Cost**: Free (open-source)

**Accuracy**: Professional-grade; peer-reviewed

**Agrivoltaic Application**: Integrated financial modeling with multiple generation sources

### Python-Based Financial Modeling (Advanced)

#### Python Libraries for Financial Analysis:
- **NumPy/Pandas**: Data manipulation and calculations
- **NumPy_IRR**: IRR calculations
- **Matplotlib**: Data visualization
- **QuantLib**: Advanced financial calculations

**Advantages**:
- Unlimited customization
- Batch processing of multiple scenarios
- Integration with optimization algorithms
- Publication-quality visualizations

**Disadvantages**:
- Steep learning curve
- Requires programming experience
- Setup complexity

**Best For**: Researchers; large-scale portfolio analysis; academic projects

**Example Script** (Simplified IRR calculation in Python):

```python
import numpy as np
from numpy_irr import NPV, IRR

# Cash flows (year 0 to year 25)
cash_flows = np.array([-600000, 180350, 180350, 182350, 182350, 184350,
                        185350, 186350, 187350, 188350, 189350, 190350,
                        191350, 192350, 193350, 194350, 195350, 196350,
                        197350, 198350, 199350, 200350, 201350, 202350,
                        203350, 210000])

# Calculate NPV at 6% discount rate
npv = NPV(0.06, cash_flows)
print(f"NPV at 6%: €{npv:,.0f}")

# Calculate IRR
irr = IRR(cash_flows)
print(f"IRR: {irr*100:.1f}%")

# Payback period
cumulative = np.cumsum(cash_flows)
payback_year = np.where(cumulative > 0)[0][0]
print(f"Payback period: {payback_year} years")
```

## 4.3.5 Step-by-Step Modeling Process

### Phase 1: Data Gathering (Weeks 1-2)

**Site & Technical Data**:
- [ ] GPS coordinates and solar irradiance (PVGIS, NREL)
- [ ] Site area and topography
- [ ] Panel specifications (efficiency, cost, warranty)
- [ ] Inverter specifications and costs
- [ ] Mounting system design and costs
- [ ] Electrical infrastructure requirements

**Agricultural Data**:
- [ ] Soil characteristics and previous yields
- [ ] Planned crop/livestock species
- [ ] Historical market prices (last 3-5 years)
- [ ] Regional production benchmarks (Agreste, CIALFO)
- [ ] Labor availability and costs

**Financial Data**:
- [ ] Loan interest rates (bank quotes)
- [ ] Government subsidy programs and rates
- [ ] System installation timeline
- [ ] Project lifetime assumptions (typically 25-30 years)

---

### Phase 2: Model Building (Weeks 3-4)

**Structure**:
1. Create Excel workbook with sheets: Assumptions, Cash Flow, Income, Metrics
2. Input all assumptions with clear labeling
3. Build year-by-year cash flow projections
4. Link all calculations to assumption sheet (enables sensitivity)
5. Calculate key metrics (NPV, IRR, payback)

**Baseline Scenario**:
- Use most likely assumptions (not best-case or worst-case)
- Document sources for all inputs

---

### Phase 3: Sensitivity Analysis (Week 5)

**Test Key Assumptions**:
1. **Critical assumptions**: FIT rate, system cost, agricultural yield, market price
2. **Create sensitivity tables**: ±20% variations
3. **Identify breakeven points**: At what assumptions does project fail?
4. **Stress test**: Combine pessimistic assumptions

**Decision Framework**:
- If IRR > 12% under pessimistic scenario: Robust project
- If IRR 8-12% under pessimistic scenario: Acceptable risk
- If IRR < 8% under pessimistic scenario: High risk

---

### Phase 4: Scenario Planning (Week 6)

**Three Scenarios**:

1. **Conservative Case**:
   - Lower solar generation (-10%)
   - Lower agricultural prices (-20%)
   - Higher operating costs (+15%)
   - Expected outcome: IRR 6-8%

2. **Base Case**:
   - Middle-of-road assumptions
   - Expected outcome: IRR 12-15%

3. **Optimistic Case**:
   - Higher solar generation (+10%)
   - Higher agricultural prices (+20%)
   - Lower operating costs (-10%)
   - Expected outcome: IRR 16-20%

**Use Case**: Present to lenders/investors with range of outcomes

---

### Phase 5: Presentation (Week 7)

**Deliverables**:
- Executive summary (1 page): Key metrics, recommendation
- Detailed financial model (spreadsheet)
- Sensitivity analysis charts
- Risk assessment matrix
- Scenario comparison table
- Technical appendix (assumptions details)

## 4.3.6 Common Modeling Errors and How to Avoid Them

| Error | Impact | Prevention |
|-------|--------|-----------|
| **Ignoring tax implications** | Overstate after-tax returns | Include corporate/personal taxes in model |
| **Underestimating OpEx** | Profit lower than projected | Use regional benchmarks; add 10% contingency |
| **Not accounting for inflation** | Real costs/revenues diverge from nominal | Index all costs/prices to 2-3% annual inflation |
| **Optimistic generation estimates** | Electricity revenue overstated | Use PVGIS conservative estimates; subtract 5% |
| **Neglecting degradation** | Long-term revenue overstated | Panel degradation 0.5-0.7%/year; inverter replacement year 12 |
| **Overestimating agricultural yields** | Revenue fails to materialize | Use regional averages; new farmers start 10-20% below target |
| **Ignoring financing costs** | Project appears profitable when not | Include interest, principal, loan fees in cash flows |
| **Wrong discount rate** | Incorrect NPV/IRR comparison | Verify discount rate reflects project risk |
| **Circular references in formulas** | Model won't calculate or gives errors | Structure dependencies: assumptions → calculations → results |
