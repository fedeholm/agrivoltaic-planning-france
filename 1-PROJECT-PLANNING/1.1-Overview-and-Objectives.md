# 1. PROJECT PLANNING

## Overview
Project planning is the foundational phase for agrivoltaic systems. This section provides frameworks for defining clear objectives, assessing site suitability, and conducting preliminary feasibility checks. Proper planning reduces risks and improves project success rates.

---

## 1.1 Overview and Objectives

### Purpose
Define the strategic vision, goals, and constraints for your agrivoltaic project before investing time and resources in detailed design.

### Key Questions to Answer

1. **Primary Objective**: What is your main goal?
   - Maximize renewable energy production
   - Develop integrated agricultural income
   - Achieve biodiversity and conservation goals
   - Create a demonstration/pilot project
   - Combination of the above

2. **Scope Definition**:
   - Land area available (hectares)
   - Current land use
   - Existing infrastructure
   - Access to utilities and grid

3. **Timeline Expectations**:
   - When do you want to start construction?
   - Expected operational date
   - Planning and permitting timeline (typically 12-24 months in France)

4. **Financial Capacity**:
   - Available capital investment
   - Access to financing/loans
   - Expected payback period expectations
   - Annual revenue targets

5. **Agricultural Focus**:
   - Primary agricultural activity (crops, trees, animals)
   - Intended products and markets
   - Existing farming experience
   - Staffing/labor availability

### Project Objectives Framework

Use the SMART criteria to define objectives:

| Criterion | Example |
|-----------|---------|
| **Specific** | "Generate 500 kW solar capacity while maintaining productive agricultural output" |
| **Measurable** | "Achieve 80% of baseline crop yields under panel shade; produce 400 MWh/year electricity" |
| **Achievable** | "Based on similar projects in similar climate zones" |
| **Relevant** | "Aligns with local agricultural practices and renewable energy policy" |
| **Time-bound** | "Operational by Q3 2027; payback by 2035" |

### Stakeholder Identification

Document all parties involved:
- **Owner/Operator**: Who owns the land and operates the system?
- **Investors/Financiers**: Banks, equity investors, government grants
- **Agricultural Partners**: Tenant farmers, contractors, input suppliers
- **Local Authorities**: Municipality, prefecture, environmental agencies
- **Neighbors**: Affected landowners, community
- **End Users**: Energy buyers, agricultural product buyers
- **Consultants**: Engineers, agronomists, environmental specialists

## 1.2 Site Assessment Framework

### Site Selection Criteria

Agrivoltaic success depends heavily on site characteristics. Use this matrix to evaluate potential sites:

#### A. Solar Resource Assessment

| Factor | Criteria | Notes |
|--------|----------|-------|
| **Annual Irradiance** | 1000-1300 kWh/m²/year | France ranges 900-1400 kWh/m²/year |
| **Latitude** | 42°N - 51°N (France) | Southern France (>1100 kWh/m²/year) preferred |
| **Shading** | <20% annual loss | Analyze seasonal sun paths, tree lines, structures |
| **Horizon** | Open view to south | Avoid valley locations; elevated sites better |
| **Micro-climate** | Stable weather patterns | Avoid fog-prone areas or high wind zones |

**Tools for Assessment**:
- NASA PVGIS database (https://pvgis.geo.esa.int/)
- PVGIS provides hourly irradiance data for any France location
- SunEarthTools for sun path analysis
- Local weather station data from Météo-France

#### B. Agricultural Suitability

| Factor | Assessment Points |
|--------|------------------|
| **Soil Quality** | Clay/loam suitable; requires >30cm depth for crops |
| **Water Availability** | Annual precipitation >600mm; irrigation potential |
| **Existing Vegetation** | Adaptable to panel shade; pasture/grassland easier |
| **Slope** | <15% ideal; <25% feasible for mounting |
| **Frost Pockets** | Avoid low-lying areas with temperature inversions |
| **Access/Roads** | Vehicles needed for maintenance and harvesting |

**Soil Testing Checklist**:
- Organic matter content (target >3%)
- pH level (6.0-7.5 optimal)
- Nutrient status (N, P, K analysis)
- Texture and structure
- Drainage characteristics
- Contaminant screening (if industrial history)

#### C. Infrastructure and Access

| Requirement | Details |
|------------|---------|
| **Grid Connection** | Distance to nearest substation; grid capacity |
| **Road Access** | Vehicles for construction, maintenance, harvest |
| **Water Supply** | Municipal supply, wells, or rainwater systems |
| **Electricity Access** | Temporary power for construction; control systems |
| **Storage/Facilities** | Space for equipment, tools, product storage |
| **Labor Availability** | Local workforce; proximity to towns |

#### D. Regulatory and Environmental Context

| Aspect | Check |
|--------|-------|
| **Zoning** | Agricultural, commercial, protected areas? |
| **Natura 2000** | Designated habitat protection areas? |
| **Flood Risk** | PPRI (Plan de Prévention du Risque Inondation) |
| **Protected Species** | Nesting birds, bats, amphibians? |
| **Air Quality** | Industrial emissions or pollution? |
| **Noise Limits** | Distance from sensitive receptors |

#### E. Land Tenure and Rights

| Question | Impact |
|----------|--------|
| **Ownership Status** | Own vs. lease; long-term secure tenure required |
| **Lease Duration** | Minimum 20-25 years for viability |
| **Right-of-Way** | Easements for power lines, roads, water |
| **Existing Contracts** | Agricultural leases, timber rights, hunting rights |
| **Succession Plans** | Family/inheritance implications |

### Site Assessment Scoring

Create a weighted scoring matrix (0-10 scale for each factor):

```
Score = Σ(Factor Score × Weight)

Example Weighting:
- Solar Resource:        30% (critical for viability)
- Agricultural Potential: 25% (revenue diversity)
- Infrastructure Access:  20% (reduces costs)
- Regulatory/Environmental: 15% (compliance risk)
- Land Tenure:            10% (reduces ownership risk)

Interpretation:
8.5-10.0  → Excellent site, proceed with detailed studies
7.0-8.5   → Good site, address specific constraints
5.5-7.0   → Moderate site, significant constraints to manage
<5.5      → Poor site, consider alternatives
```

### Baseline Data Collection

Document current conditions:

1. **Topographical Survey**
   - Elevation changes (DEM from IGN/Geoportail)
   - Drainage patterns
   - Aspect (compass direction of slope)

2. **Vegetation Mapping**
   - Current land cover types
   - Tree species and heights
   - Seasonal patterns

3. **Photographic Record**
   - Season photos (spring, summer, fall, winter)
   - Sun angle documentation
   - Neighboring land use

4. **Climate Data** (from Météo-France or similar)
   - Average annual precipitation
   - Temperature range (min/max monthly)
   - Wind speed and direction
   - Frost dates and growing season length

5. **Agricultural History**
   - Previous crop types and yields
   - Fertilizer and pesticide use
   - Pest/disease history
   - Market access and infrastructure

## 1.3 Feasibility Checklist

### Pre-Project Go/No-Go Decision Framework

Use this checklist to determine if a project should proceed to detailed design phase.

#### ✅ FINANCIAL FEASIBILITY

- [ ] Estimated project cost <€3,000-5,000/kW (typical France range)
- [ ] Projected revenue covers all costs within acceptable timeframe (15-25 years)
- [ ] Access to financing (bank loans, equity, grants)
- [ ] Margin for cost overruns (recommend 20-30% contingency)
- [ ] Annual operating budget identified and realistic
- [ ] Multiple revenue streams identified (energy + agricultural products)

**Financial Red Flags**:
- Project cost >€6,000/kW without compelling research/pilot justification
- Payback >30 years
- Single revenue stream (too risky)
- No contingency budget

#### ✅ TECHNICAL FEASIBILITY

- [ ] Solar irradiance >950 kWh/m²/year (minimum for viability)
- [ ] Grid connection possible (within 5 km ideally)
- [ ] Mounting system suitable for soil type and slope
- [ ] Agricultural operations compatible with panel infrastructure
- [ ] Water/irrigation management plan feasible
- [ ] Access for construction vehicles and ongoing maintenance

**Technical Red Flags**:
- Irradiance <900 kWh/m²/year
- Grid connection >15 km or requires major infrastructure
- Extremely rocky or waterlogged soil
- >30° slope (complex mounting)

#### ✅ AGRICULTURAL FEASIBILITY

- [ ] Selected crops/animals viable under panel shade (shade tolerance confirmed)
- [ ] Soil quality supports intended production
- [ ] Water availability adequate for both panels (cleaning) and crops
- [ ] Local markets exist for agricultural products
- [ ] Labor availability for seasonal work
- [ ] Knowledge/experience or training plan in place

**Agricultural Red Flags**:
- No local market for products
- Chronic water scarcity
- Shade-intolerant crops (many cereals not suitable)
- Extremely poor soil requiring major remediation

#### ✅ REGULATORY/ENVIRONMENTAL FEASIBILITY

- [ ] No Natura 2000 designations or conflicts manageable
- [ ] Zoning allows agrivoltaic use
- [ ] Permitting pathway clear (solar + agricultural)
- [ ] Environmental impact acceptable (biodiversity, water, air)
- [ ] Protected species assessment completed (if needed)
- [ ] No major opposition from neighbors/community

**Regulatory Red Flags**:
- Core Natura 2000 site with sensitive habitats
- Zoning prohibits commercial solar
- Major archaeological sites
- Severe environmental constraints

#### ✅ OPERATIONAL FEASIBILITY

- [ ] Operator has or can acquire necessary expertise
- [ ] Staffing/labor model sustainable long-term
- [ ] Equipment suppliers and service providers accessible
- [ ] Maintenance schedule realistic and budgeted
- [ ] Risk management plan addresses key hazards
- [ ] Insurance products available at reasonable cost

**Operational Red Flags**:
- Operator has no solar or agricultural experience; no mentoring available
- Remote location with no nearby service providers
- Specialized equipment with long lead times
- Limited insurance options

#### ✅ STAKEHOLDER & COMMUNITY ACCEPTANCE

- [ ] Landowner fully committed (if different from operator)
- [ ] Local authorities supportive or neutral
- [ ] Neighbors informed; major opposition absent
- [ ] Agricultural association/chamber of commerce consulted
- [ ] Environmental NGOs engaged (if relevant)
- [ ] Potential tenant farmers or contractors identified

**Acceptance Red Flags**:
- Organized opposition from neighbors
- Landowner uncertain or conflicted
- Local authorities hostile to solar development
- Community concerns unaddressed

#### ✅ STRATEGIC ALIGNMENT

- [ ] Project aligns with owner's long-term vision
- [ ] Complements existing agricultural operations (if applicable)
- [ ] Fits regional/national policy objectives (eligible for support)
- [ ] Market timing favorable (energy/agricultural)
- [ ] Exit strategy clear (resale, continued operation, etc.)

### Next Steps if Feasibility Confirmed

If your feasibility assessment is positive:

1. **Commission professional studies**:
   - Detailed solar resource assessment (PV design engineer)
   - Soil and agricultural feasibility study (agronomist)
   - Environmental impact assessment (if required)
   - Structural engineering for mounting systems

2. **Obtain initial permits/approvals**:
   - Request for preliminary zoning opinion (avis de légalité)
   - Grid connection request to local DSO (Enedis/etc.)
   - Environmental screening decision

3. **Establish project team**:
   - Find experienced agrivoltaic design consultant
   - Hire legal advisors for contracts and regulations
   - Identify potential agricultural partners/tenants

4. **Develop detailed financial model** (→ Section 4)

5. **Proceed to Section 2**: Technical systems design
