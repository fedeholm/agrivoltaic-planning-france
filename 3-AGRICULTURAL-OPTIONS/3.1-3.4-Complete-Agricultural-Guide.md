# 3. AGRICULTURAL OPTIONS

## Overview
This section explores the diverse agricultural activities that can be integrated with solar PV systems in agrivoltaic projects. Success depends on selecting species and practices compatible with partial shade, meeting market demand, and fitting regional conditions in France. This section covers crops, orchards, trees, and small animal husbandry options with practical guidance for each.

---

## 3.1 Crop Integration

### Introduction to Shade-Tolerant Cropping

Crops under solar panels experience 30-60% less direct sunlight depending on configuration. Success requires selecting shade-tolerant species and optimizing agronomic practices for reduced light conditions.

### Shade Tolerance Classification

**Light Requirement Levels**:
- **Full Sun Required** (>6 hours direct sun): Corn, sunflower, soybean, wheat, barley
  - ❌ NOT suitable for agrivoltaics
- **Partial Shade Tolerant** (4-6 hours sun): Many vegetables, some grains, forage crops
  - ✅ Good for agrivoltaic systems
- **Shade Tolerant** (2-4 hours sun): Salad greens, herbs, some berries, mushrooms
  - ✅ Excellent for dense agrivoltaic arrays
- **Shade Loving** (<2 hours sun): Mushrooms, leafy greens in high-shade systems
  - ✅ Ideal for dense overhead panels (>50% shade)

### Suitable Crops for French Agrivoltaics

#### A. Leafy Greens and Vegetables (40-60% Shade Tolerance)

| Crop | Shade Tolerance | Yield Impact | Market Value | French Regions | Special Notes |
|------|-----------------|--------------|--------------|----------------|---------------|
| **Lettuce** | 50-60% | -20-30% (lighter greens) | €0.50-1.20/kg | All regions | Year-round with protection |
| **Spinach** | 50-60% | -15-25% | €0.80-1.50/kg | All regions | Spring/fall optimal |
| **Arugula (Roquette)** | 50-60% | -20-30% | €1.00-2.00/kg | All regions | Premium market potential |
| **Chard (Blette)** | 50-60% | -25-35% | €0.60-1.20/kg | All regions | Perennial in mild winters |
| **Kale** | 40-50% | -15-25% | €0.80-1.80/kg | Northern/Central | Hardier than spinach |
| **Bok Choy** | 50-60% | -20-30% | €0.70-1.50/kg | All regions | Asian market growing |
| **Endive** | 50-60% | -20-30% | €0.80-1.50/kg | Northern France | Forcing in controlled shade |
| **Chicory** | 45-55% | -20-35% | €0.70-1.40/kg | All regions | Industrial cultivars available |

**Agronomic Considerations**:
- **Reduced Bolting**: Shade delays flowering (extends harvest window for spring crops)
- **Water Savings**: 30-50% less irrigation needed (reduced evaporation)
- **Reduced Pest Pressure**: Some pests (aphids, whiteflies) less prevalent in shade
- **Quality Premium**: Tender greens with good flavor possible
- **Season Extension**: Spring and fall seasons longer (cooler microclimate)

**Production Model** (1 hectare, 40% shade):
- Succession plantings every 3-4 weeks
- 8-10 harvests per year (vs 6-8 in full sun)
- Annual yield: 40-80 tons fresh lettuce equivalent
- Revenue: €40,000-120,000/year (farmer market to wholesale)

### Integrated Crop Rotation for Agrivoltaics

Successful shade-tolerant cropping requires planned rotation to maintain soil health and market diversity.

**Three-Year Rotation Example** (1 hectare):

| Year | Spring (Mar-May) | Summer (Jun-Aug) | Fall (Sep-Nov) |
|------|------------------|------------------|-----------------|
| **Year 1** | Lettuce succession (8 crops) | Herbs (mint, parsley, chives) | Spinach, chard succession |
| **Year 2** | Bok choy, Asian greens | Basil (on south side), cilantro | Kale, winter greens |
| **Year 3** | Forage/pasture build-up | Mushroom production | Forage resting period |

**Soil Health Maintenance**:
- Annual compost addition: 5-10 tons/hectare
- Cover cropping: Winter legumes (clover, vetch)
- Mulching: Organic mulch 5-10cm reduces water loss, improves soil
- Crop residue management: Compost or chop-and-drop

## 3.2 Orchard and Tree Systems

### Tree Crop Integration with Solar

Trees can be planted beneath elevated panels, creating a three-layer system: canopy (panels) → tree crop (middle) → understory (shrubs/herbaceous plants).

### Suitable Tree Species for French Agrivoltaics

#### A. Nut Trees (20-40 Year Productivity)

| Species | Shade Tolerance | Yield/Tree | Maturity | Market Value | French Regions |
|---------|-----------------|-----------|----------|--------------|-----------------|
| **Walnut (Noyer)** | 40% | 10-20 kg/year (mature) | 7-10 years | €3-6/kg | Central/Southern |
| **Hazelnut** | 50% | 2-4 kg/year | 5-8 years | €4-8/kg | Northern/Central |
| **Chestnut (Châtaigne)** | 30-40% | 5-15 kg/year | 8-12 years | €5-12/kg | Central/Southern |
| **Almond** | 20-30% | 2-4 kg/year | 5-8 years | €8-12/kg | Southern only |

**Agronomic Considerations**:
- **Spacing**: 8-10m between trees (allows solar panels above)
- **Underplanting**: Herbs, shrubs in first 5 years (before tree canopy closes)
- **Shade Development**: Panels initially shaded less; more shade after 10-15 years (plan accordingly)
- **Pollination**: Many nut trees need cross-pollination (plant 2+ varieties)

**Economic Model** (Walnut orchard with solar, 1 hectare):
- Tree cost + planting: €5,000-8,000
- Solar system cost: €300,000-400,000
- Year 1-6: Electricity only (€40,000-60,000/year)
- Year 7-15: Electricity + walnut sales increasing (€40,000 + €20,000-40,000)
- Year 15+: Mature system (€40,000 + €60,000-100,000)
- Total 30-year revenue: €1.8M-2.5M

**Production Systems**:

  - Low management; lower yield

**Economic Model** (Apple orchard + solar, 1 hectare, 250 trees):

- Year 6-15: Full production (€40,000 + €40,000-80,000)
- Year 15+: Sustained (€40,000 + €40,000-80,000)

**Value-Added Opportunities**:
- Fresh apple sales: €0.80-1.20/kg
- Cider production: €2-4/liter (premium cider €5-8/bottle)
- Dried/processed: €3-6/kg

## 3.3 Small Animal Husbandry

Livestock integration maximizes land-use efficiency, provides ecosystem services (weed control, fertilization), and diversifies income streams.

### Suitable Livestock for Agrivoltaic Systems

#### A. Sheep (Optimal for France)

**Characteristics**:
- **Panel Height Requirement**: 1.5-2.5m clearance (sheep height ~1.5m)
- **Stocking Density**: 4-8 sheep per hectare under panels (vs 8-12 in full sun)
- **Primary Products**: Wool, meat, milk (if dairy breed)
- **Maintenance Function**: Natural grazing controls weeds; reduces mowing costs

**Breed Selection for France**:

| Breed | Purpose | Size | Productivity | Suited Region |
|-------|---------|------|--------------|---------------|
| **Mérinos** | Wool | Medium | 4-6 kg/year | All regions |
| **Charolais** | Meat | Large | 25-35 kg/year (lamb) | Central/Southern |
| **Limousine** | Meat | Large | 28-38 kg/year (lamb) | Central/Southern |
| **Lacaune** | Dairy | Medium | 200-300L/year | South |
| **Comtoise** | Mixed | Medium | Balanced milk/meat | Eastern |
| **Romney** | Wool/meat | Medium | 5-6 kg wool + meat | Wetter regions |

**Grazing Management Under Panels**:
- **Rotational Grazing**: Move sheep every 2-4 weeks to allow forage recovery
- **Rest Period**: Minimum 6 weeks between grazing cycles
- **Fencing**: Portable electric fencing (€2,000-5,000 for 5 hectares)
- **Water Supply**: Troughs moved with flock (€500-2,000)
- **Shelter**: Moveable shelters or external housing

**Economic Model** (100 sheep + 10 hectare agrivoltaic site):
- Initial sheep cost: €3,000-5,000 (€30-50 per sheep)
- Annual feed cost: €2,000-3,000 (supplemental to grazing)
- Shepherd labor: €15,000-25,000/year (part-time possible)
- Annual revenue:
  - Wool: 20,000 kg @ €0.60/kg = €12,000
  - Meat (lambs): 40 lambs @ €180/lamb = €7,200
  - Milk (if dairy): 15,000L @ €0.45/L = €6,750 (dairy breeds only)
  - Vegetation management savings: €8,000-12,000 (no mowing contractor)
- **Total Annual Revenue**: €27,200-37,950
- **Net Profit**: €7,200-17,950 (after costs)

**Environmental Benefits**:
- ✅ Reduced herbicide use (sheep grazing controls weeds)
- ✅ Improved soil fertility (manure distribution)
- ✅ Enhanced biodiversity (varied grazing patterns)
- ✅ Carbon sequestration (grazing lands)

#### B. Goats (Good for Rough Terrain)

**Characteristics**:
- **Panel Height Requirement**: 1.5-2m clearance (similar to sheep)
- **Stocking Density**: 3-6 goats per hectare
- **Primary Products**: Milk, cheese, meat, fiber (angora/cashmere breeds)
- **Unique Advantage**: Browse shrubs and woody plants (not just grass)

**Breed Selection**:

| Breed | Purpose | Production | Market Value |
|-------|---------|-----------|--------------|
| **Alpine** | Dairy | 2-3 kg milk/day | Premium (cheese) |
| **Saanen** | Dairy | 2-4 kg milk/day | Premium (cheese) |
| **Angora** | Fiber | 0.8-1.5 kg/year (mohair) | €15-30/kg |
| **Boer** | Meat | 25-35 kg/year (kids) | €150-300/animal |
| **Cashmere** | Fiber + meat | 0.15-0.2 kg/year (cashmere) | €30-100/kg |

**Advantages Over Sheep**:
- ✅ Browse control (reduce shrub encroachment)
- ✅ Milk/cheese premium market (higher value)
- ✅ Fiber production (mohair, cashmere)
- ❌ Higher management requirement (more escape risk, parasite susceptibility)

**Economic Model** (50 dairy goats + 5 hectare site):
- Initial cost: €3,000-5,000 (€60-100 per goat)
- Annual operating costs: €8,000-12,000
- Annual production: 30,000L milk @ €0.80/L = €24,000
- Or cheese production: 6,000 kg cheese @ €8-15/kg = €48,000-90,000
- **Net Profit (milk): €12,000-16,000/year**
- **Net Profit (cheese): €36,000-78,000/year** (more processing/marketing effort)

#### C. Cattle (Requires Higher Clearance)

**Characteristics**:
- **Panel Height Requirement**: 2.5-3m clearance minimum (cattle ~2.2m tall)
- **Stocking Density**: 1-2 cattle per hectare under panels (vs 2-4 in full sun)
- **Primary Products**: Meat, milk
- **Manure**: Significant fertilizer production

**Breed Selection**:

| Breed | Purpose | Production | French Regions |
|-------|---------|-----------|-----------------|
| **Charolais** | Beef | 600-700 kg carcass | Central/Southern |
| **Limousine** | Beef | 550-650 kg carcass | Central |
| **Salers** | Beef/Dairy | Dual-purpose | Central (mountains) |
| **Aubrac** | Beef | 600-700 kg carcass | Central (mountains) |
| **Normandy** | Dairy | 5,500-7,000L/year | Normandy/Brittany |
| **Montbéliarde** | Dairy | 5,000-6,500L/year | Eastern (Jura) |

**Grazing Management**:
- **Rotational Grazing**: Longer rest periods (8-12 weeks) than sheep
- **Forage Quality**: Cattle need better forage (less selective than sheep/goats)
- **Trampling Risk**: May damage panels or infrastructure if not properly managed
- **Water/Salt Troughs**: Essential; require moving with herds

**Economic Model** (20 beef cattle + 20 hectare site):
- Initial cattle cost: €4,000-6,000 (€200-300 per animal)
- Annual feed costs: €3,000-5,000
- Labor: €10,000-15,000/year
- Annual revenue (finishing 8 calves/year): 4 tons meat @ €7-10/kg = €28,000-40,000
- **Net Profit: €13,000-22,000/year** (substantial labor required)

**Note**: Cattle under panels requires careful planning; typically limited to spaces with >2.5m clearance. Mixed sheep + cattle systems can be effective (sheep graze fine herbaceous plants; cattle graze coarser vegetation).

**Characteristics**:
- **Panel Height**: Flexible (chickens <1m, duck/geese <1.5m)

- **Primary Products**: Eggs, meat, pest control
- **Manure Production**: Significant fertilizer (high nitrogen)

**Species and Production**:

| Species | Primary Product | Annual Production | Market Value | Notes |

| **Turkeys** | Meat | 1 bird/year, 5-8 kg meat | €8-15/kg | Premium market |

**Advantages for Agrivoltaics**:
- ✅ Minimal footprint requirement
- ✅ Pest management (eat insects, grubs)
- ✅ High-value eggs/meat
- ✅ Mobile housing enables rotational use
- ✅ Excellent forage converters (grass → eggs/meat)

**Economic Model** (200 laying hens + 2 hectare site):

**Integration with Crops**:
- Mobile chicken coops rotated through cropped areas

- Manure fertilizes next cropping area
- Creates pest-suppression cycle

#### E. Bees (Apiaries)

**Characteristics**:
- **Space Requirement**: Minimal (hives occupy <1% land area)
- **Primary Products**: Honey, pollen, propolis, wax, pollination service
- **Pollination Benefit**: Improves crop yields (especially fruits, nuts)
- **Panel Compatibility**: Excellent (bees use panels as windbreaks)

**Apiary Economics**:
- **Hive Cost**: €150-300 per hive (equipment + bees)
- **Apiary Size**: 10-50 hives per hectare (more with flower resources)
- **Annual Honey Production**: 15-30 kg per hive
- **Market Price**: €8-20/kg (premium honey €15-40/kg)
- **Annual Revenue (20 hives)**: 300-600 kg honey @ €12/kg = €3,600-7,200
- **Labor**: Minimal (seasonal management)

**Pollination Benefits**:
- Fruit/nut tree sets improve 20-30%
- Vegetable pollination improves (especially cucurbits, brassicas)
- Estimated value: €1,000-5,000/year (indirect benefit)

**Best Placement**:
- Perimeter locations (not under panels)
- Near flowering plants, crops for forage
- South-facing edge preferred (sun-loving bees)
- Windbreak benefit from panels

**Synergies**:
- Bees pollinate shade-tolerant crops (berries, vegetables)
- Panels provide shelter/protection
- Reduced pesticide use improves bee health
- Organic certification often requires bee-friendly practices

| **Northern France** (Nord, Pas-de-Calais) | Temperate oceanic | Apr-Oct (180 days) | Grains, dairy, brassicas | Cool-adapted |
| **Normandy/Brittany** | Oceanic | Apr-Oct (190 days) | Dairy, apples, vegetables | Wet; good for perennials |
| **Eastern France** (Alsace, Jura) | Continental | Apr-Oct (160 days) | Cereals, timber, fruit | Cold winters; frost risk |
| **Central France** (Loire Valley) | Temperate | Apr-Nov (200 days) | Diverse (cereals, fruit, wine) | Excellent agrivoltaic potential |
| **Southern France** (Provence, Occitanie) | Mediterranean | Mar-Nov (240+ days) | Almonds, citrus, olives, grapes | Drought risk; heat stress |
| **Alpine Regions** (Massif Central, Alps) | Mountain/Continental | May-Sep (120-150 days) | Pasture, timber, hardy crops | Short season; sheep/goat pastoral |

### Regional Species Recommendations

#### Northern France (Hauts-de-France, Normandy, Brittany)
**Best Crops**:
- Leafy greens (lettuce, spinach, kale)
- Brassicas (cabbage, broccoli when shade-tolerant varieties)
- Berries (blackcurrant, gooseberry, raspberry)

**Best Livestock**:
- Dairy cattle (Normandy breed native)
- Sheep (wool varieties)
- Dairy goats

**Best Trees**:
- Apple/pear orchards
- Timber species (oak, ash, beech)
- Alder (wet areas)

**Strategic Advantage**: Strong dairy market; established organic infrastructure

---

#### Central France (Loire Valley, Allier, Puy-de-Dôme)
**Best Crops**:
- Vegetables (full range of shade-tolerant species)
- Herbs (premium market in Paris region)
- Mushrooms (climate-controlled systems)

**Best Livestock**:
- Beef cattle (Charolais, Limousine)
- Sheep (mixed breeds)
- Poultry (eggs for regional market)

**Best Trees**:
- Walnut orchards
- Cherry orchards
- Mixed timber species

**Strategic Advantage**: Central location; excellent proximity to Paris market; established agritourism; strong agricultural heritage

---

#### Southern France (Occitanie, Provence, Nouvelle-Aquitaine)
**Best Crops**:
- Herbs (rosemary, thyme, lavender)
- Wine grapes (some shade-tolerant varieties)
- Almonds (on south-facing slopes)

**Best Livestock**:
- Sheep (Mediterranean breeds)
- Goats (milk/cheese)
- Bees (excellent forage)

**Best Trees**:
- Almond orchards
- Walnut (more vigorous than north)
- Olive (experimental with shade-panels)

**Strategic Advantage**: Premium markets (herbs, wine, organic); strong tourism sector; Mediterranean products

---

#### Eastern France (Alsace, Burgundy, Jura)
**Best Crops**:
- Timber production (oak, beech optimal)
- Wine (cooler-climate varieties; some shade-compatible)
- Berries (especially currants, gooseberries)

**Best Livestock**:
- Cattle (Montbéliarde for dairy; beef cattle)
- Sheep (pasture-based systems)
- Bees (good forage availability)

**Best Trees**:
- Oak and beech timber
- Cherry orchards (famous Jura cherries)
- Chestnut (southern Burgundy)

**Strategic Advantage**: Strong cheese/dairy market; wine regions; established forest management

---

#### Alpine/Mountain Regions (Massif Central, Pyrénées, Alps)
**Best Crops**:
- Forage and pasture (primary land use)
- Berries (hardy varieties)
- Timber (conifers, hardy deciduous)

**Best Livestock**:
- Sheep (pastoral foundation; wool + meat)
- Goats (browse management; dairy potential)
- Cattle (beef-focused; hardy breeds)

**Best Trees**:
- Pine/conifer species
- Oak and beech (lower elevations)
- Poplar (sheltered valleys)

**Strategic Advantage**: Strong pastoral heritage; organic certification potential; carbon credit opportunities; biodiversity value

### Agricultural Risk Assessment

**Crop Production Risks by Region**:

| Risk Factor | Northern | Central | Southern | Alpine |
|------------|----------|---------|----------|--------|
| **Frost Damage** | High (Apr) | Moderate | Low | Very High |
| **Drought Stress** | Low | Low-Moderate | High | Moderate |
| **Excess Rainfall** | Moderate | Low | Very Low | High (spring) |
| **Pest Pressure** | Moderate | Moderate-High | High | Low |
| **Market Access** | Good (urban) | Excellent | Good | Difficult |

**Risk Mitigation Strategies**:
- Frost: Crop timing, frost-hardy varieties, wind machines
- Drought: Irrigation, mulching, deficit irrigation techniques
- Pests: Integrated pest management, beneficial insects, reduced chemicals
- Market: Diversification, value-added processing, direct sales
