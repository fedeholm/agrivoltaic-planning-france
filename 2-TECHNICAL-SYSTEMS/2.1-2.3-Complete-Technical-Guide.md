# 2. TECHNICAL SYSTEMS

## Overview
This section covers the technical design and infrastructure components of agrivoltaic systems. It encompasses solar photovoltaic (PV) technology options, different agrivoltaic configurations, and supporting infrastructure required for successful implementation in France.

---

## 2.1 Solar PV Technologies

### Introduction to Photovoltaic Systems

Solar PV systems convert sunlight directly into electricity through the photovoltaic effect. For agrivoltaic applications, technology selection impacts both energy output and agricultural compatibility.

### Types of Solar PV Technologies

#### A. Crystalline Silicon Panels (Most Common for Agrivoltaics)

**Monocrystalline Silicon (Mono-Si)**
- **Efficiency**: 18-22% laboratory; 15-20% field performance
- **Cost**: €150-250/kW installed (2024 France prices)
- **Lifespan**: 25-30 years; 80%+ capacity after 25 years
- **Advantages**:
  - Highest efficiency (more power in less space)
  - Better temperature coefficient (performs better in heat)
  - Long proven track record
  - Widely available
- **Disadvantages**:
  - Higher initial cost than polycrystalline
  - Slightly more brittle (handling care)
- **Best for**: Space-constrained sites; maximizing output
- **Agrivoltaic suitability**: Excellent - high efficiency allows wider spacing for agricultural operations

**Polycrystalline Silicon (Poly-Si)**
- **Efficiency**: 15-18% laboratory; 13-17% field
- **Cost**: €130-200/kW installed (2024 France prices)
- **Lifespan**: 25-30 years; similar degradation to mono-Si
- **Advantages**:
  - Lower cost than monocrystalline
  - Adequate efficiency for most applications
  - Good track record
- **Disadvantages**:
  - Slightly lower efficiency (requires more panels for same output)
  - Poorer performance at high temperatures
- **Best for**: Budget-constrained projects; areas with moderate climate
- **Agrivoltaic suitability**: Good - lower cost but requires more space

#### B. Thin-Film Technologies (Emerging for Agrivoltaics)

**Cadmium Telluride (CdTe)**
- **Efficiency**: 16-18% laboratory; 12-15% field
- **Cost**: €100-150/kW (lower than crystalline)
- **Advantages**:
  - Better performance in diffuse light (cloudy weather)
  - Better high-temperature performance
  - Lower embodied energy to produce
  - Thinner, lighter modules
- **Disadvantages**:
  - Lower efficiency than crystalline (needs more area)
  - Fewer manufacturers; limited supply chain
  - Environmental concerns about cadmium (fully encapsulated, safe)
- **Best for**: Cloudy climates; integrated building applications
- **Agrivoltaic suitability**: Emerging - good for French cloudy conditions; thinner allows more light transmission for crops

**Copper Indium Gallium Selenide (CIGS)**
- **Efficiency**: 17-22% laboratory; 14-18% field
- **Cost**: €150-250/kW (similar to mono-Si)
- **Advantages**:
  - Highest efficiency potential among thin-films
  - Better diffuse light performance
  - Flexible options available
- **Disadvantages**:
  - Limited commercial availability
  - Higher cost for current production levels
- **Best for**: Research/pilot projects; specialized applications
- **Agrivoltaic suitability**: Experimental - promising but limited availability

**Perovskite (Emerging)**
- **Efficiency**: 25%+ laboratory (rapidly improving); 15-20% field (limited data)
- **Cost**: Under development; likely €100-200/kW at scale
- **Advantages**:
  - Very high potential efficiency
  - Tuneable bandgap (can optimize for specific light wavelengths)
  - Lightweight; flexible options
  - Lower manufacturing cost potential
- **Disadvantages**:
  - Not yet commercially available at scale
  - Stability concerns (durability testing ongoing)
  - Manufacturing standardization incomplete
- **Best for**: Future agrivoltaic systems; pilot demonstrations
- **Agrivoltaic suitability**: High potential - could allow wavelength-tuned panels (let red/near-IR through to plants)

#### C. Bifacial Panels (Specialized for Agrivoltaics)

**Description**: Panels that capture sunlight on both front and back surfaces

- **Additional Gain**: 10-25% extra power from reflected/scattered light
- **Cost**: €50-100/kW premium over standard panels
- **Requirements**:
  - Reflective surface below panels (white/light-colored ground)
  - Elevated mounting (≥0.5m clearance for light reflection)
  - Taller frame structures
- **Advantages**:
  - Significant output increase without more land area
  - Light reflecting from crops/grass below increases output
  - Better for diffuse light conditions
- **Disadvantages**:
  - More complex mounting system
  - Taller frames may complicate agricultural operations
  - Requires maintenance of reflective surfaces
- **Agrivoltaic suitability**: Excellent - perfectly suited for elevated systems; agricultural use below provides reflective surface

### System Components (Beyond Panels)

#### Inverters
Convert DC electricity from panels to AC electricity for grid/use

**String Inverters** (Most common for agrivoltaics)
- Single inverter for multiple panel strings
- Cost: €400-800 per unit (for 10-50 kW capacity)
- Efficiency: 97-99%
- Advantages: Simple, reliable, cost-effective
- Maintenance: Few moving parts; long lifespan (10-15 years)

**Micro-inverters** (Emerging)
- Individual inverter per panel
- Cost: €100-200 per panel (total higher per kW)
- Efficiency: 96-98%
- Advantages: Better performance with partial shading; easier troubleshooting
- Best for: Complex agricultural layouts with varying shade

**Hybrid Inverters**
- Combine grid-tie and battery functionality
- Cost: €3,000-8,000 per unit
- Enables energy storage for later use or peak demand
- For remote sites without reliable grid

#### Mounting Systems

**Fixed-Tilt Mounts**
- Panels at fixed angle (typically 25-35° in France)
- Cost: €200-400/kW
- Advantages: Simple, reliable, minimal maintenance
- Disadvantages: Fixed angle not optimal year-round
- **Agrivoltaic suitability**: Good for elevated systems; allows agricultural use below

**Tracker Systems** (Single-axis or Dual-axis)
- Panels follow sun movement
- Cost: €300-600/kW (mechanical complexity adds cost)
- Efficiency gain: 20-35% more output than fixed
- Advantages: Maximizes energy production
- Disadvantages: 
  - Moving parts require more maintenance
  - Mechanical failures possible
  - Can complicate agricultural operations below
  - Higher wind loading (may require stronger structures)
- **Agrivoltaic suitability**: Limited - mechanical complexity and moving structures complicate farming; better for rows elevated high enough

**Elevated Horizontal Systems** (Key for agrivoltaics)
- Panels at height (typically 2.5-4m) to allow machinery and animals below
- Cost: €250-500/kW (additional structure cost)
- Advantages: 
  - Preserves agricultural space below
  - Allows equipment passage
  - Can use vertical space for shade-tolerant crops
- **Agrivoltaic suitability**: Excellent - designed for integrated farming

**Ground-Level Systems**
- Panels close to ground (0.5-1m)
- Cost: €200-350/kW (minimal structure)
- Advantages: Lower cost; easier installation
- Disadvantages: 
  - Limits agricultural use below
  - Difficult machinery access
  - Pest/weed management challenges
- **Agrivoltaic suitability**: Poor - minimizes agricultural benefits

#### Wiring, Disconnects, and Safety Equipment

- **DC wiring**: Size based on current; typically €0.50-1.50/meter
- **AC wiring**: To inverter and grid connection; €1.00-2.00/meter
- **Disconnects/Breakers**: Safety switches for maintenance; €500-2,000 per system
- **Grounding**: Lightning protection; €1,000-3,000
- **Surge protection**: Equipment protection; €500-1,500

#### Monitoring and Control Systems

- **Real-time monitoring**: Track generation by string/inverter
- **Cost**: €1,000-5,000 per system
- **Benefits**: 
  - Early fault detection
  - Performance optimization
  - Data collection for analysis
- **Optional**: Remote monitoring, automated alerts, mobile app access

#### Grid Connection Equipment

- **Transformer** (if needed): Step-up voltage for grid; €2,000-10,000
- **Grid interconnection hardware**: Safety disconnect, protection; €2,000-8,000
- **Metering**: Bidirectional meters to track export; included in grid contract

## 2.2 Agrivoltaic Configurations

### Introduction to System Layouts

The spatial arrangement of solar panels relative to agricultural operations is critical for agrivoltaic success. Different configurations offer different tradeoffs between solar output and agricultural productivity.

### Configuration Types

#### A. Elevated Horizontal Systems (Recommended for France)

**Description**: Panels mounted on tall structures (2.5-4.0m clearance) in horizontal arrays, allowing farming below

**Characteristics**:
- **Spacing**: Rows spaced 4-8m apart (allows tractor passage)
- **Panel Orientation**: South-facing (France); typically 25-35° tilt
- **Ground Coverage Ratio (GCR)**: 30-50% (30-50% of land area under panels)
- **Light Transmission**: 30-50% reaches ground below
- **Land Use Intensity**: High - dual use of same land area

**Advantages**:
- ✅ Preserves most farmable land
- ✅ Allows standard machinery passage
- ✅ Full crop/animal operations possible below
- ✅ Excellent for France with diverse agriculture
- ✅ Bifacial panel benefits

**Disadvantages**:
- ❌ Higher structural cost (taller frames, stronger foundations)
- ❌ Wind loading concerns (taller structures)
- ❌ Slightly lower panel efficiency (more diffuse light to panels)

**Best For**:
- Diverse crop rotations
- Livestock grazing (sheep, goats, cattle under panels)
- Medium to large sites (>5 hectares)
- Regions with moderate wind

**Case Study Example**: 
Ferme Agrivoltaïque du Tarn (France) - 1.5 MW system with 2.5m clearance; sheep grazing below; wheat rotation; +€80,000/year from wool/meat + electricity

---

#### B. Vertical/Angled Row Systems

**Description**: Panels arranged in vertical or highly angled rows, often north-south oriented, creating alternating sun/shade zones

**Characteristics**:
- **Orientation**: Typically north-south rows; creates east-west sun/shade pattern
- **Row Height**: 2-3m at peak
- **Panel Angle**: Vertical or 45-60° angle
- **Ground Coverage Ratio**: 30-50%
- **Light Transmission**: 40-60% between rows

**Advantages**:
- ✅ Excellent light distribution (more uniform under panels)
- ✅ Reduced shading (sun reaches between rows throughout day)
- ✅ Better for shade-sensitive crops (some vegetables)
- ✅ Aesthetically less imposing
- ✅ Good for medium-height crops and grazing

**Disadvantages**:
- ❌ Slightly lower total energy output (sub-optimal angle)
- ❌ More complex tracking if dynamic
- ❌ Wind effects on tall structures

**Best For**:
- Shade-sensitive crop production
- Vegetable/herb cultivation
- Higher-value crops (where premium for "solar-grown" justified)
- Sloped terrain

**French Regional Suitability**: Good for vegetable-growing regions (Loire Valley, Provence)

---

#### C. Tree/Orchard Integration (Agro-Forestry Hybrid)

**Description**: Solar panels integrated with tree rows (fruit/nut trees, timber, agroforestry species)

**Characteristics**:
- **Panel Height**: 2.5-4m (above tree canopy or between rows)
- **Tree Spacing**: Typical tree rows 5-8m apart; panels mounted above
- **Ground Coverage**: 20-40% (panel array)
- **Tree Canopy**: 30-40% of land area shaded by trees
- **Total Shade**: 50-70% of land (panels + trees)

**Advantages**:
- ✅ Three income streams: electricity + tree products + understory crops
- ✅ Enhanced biodiversity (trees provide habitat)
- ✅ Wind protection (trees as windbreaks)
- ✅ Improved soil health (trees' deep roots, leaf litter)
- ✅ Carbon sequestration (timber/biomass)
- ✅ Longer project lifespan (trees add permanent value)

**Disadvantages**:
- ❌ Complex management (coordinating two growth cycles)
- ❌ Initial tree establishment period (3-5 years before substantial growth)
- ❌ Tree shade reduces panel output initially; benefit increases over time
- ❌ Higher initial investment (trees + panels)

**Best For**:
- Long-term projects (20+ years)
- Regions with wood/fruit market demand
- Carbon credit projects
- Conservation-focused initiatives

**Tree Species for France**:
- **Nut Trees**: Walnut (Juglans regia), Chestnut (Castanea), Hazelnut (Corylus)
- **Timber**: Oak (Quercus), Ash (Fraxinus), Beech (Fagus)
- **Fruit**: Cherry (Prunus), Apple/Pear (Malus/Pyrus), Plum (Prunus domestica)
- **Agroforestry**: Alder (Alnus), Poplar (Populus), Willow (Salix)

**Example Revenue**: Walnut orchard + solar at 1 MW system
- Year 1-5: €120,000/year from electricity only
- Year 6-15: €120,000 + €20,000 (walnut harvest)
- Year 15+: €120,000 + €60,000+ (mature walnut production)

---

#### D. Horizontal Trackers (Single-Axis)

**Description**: Panels on systems that rotate east-west to follow sun throughout day

**Characteristics**:
- **Rotation**: 120-150° daily rotation (east to west)
- **Clearance**: Highest point typically 3-4m
- **Ground Coverage**: 20-30% (more land exposed)
- **Energy Gain**: 20-35% more output than fixed systems
- **Complexity**: Mechanical moving parts

**Advantages**:
- ✅ Significantly higher energy production
- ✅ Excellent light distribution to crops (tracking reduces overhead shade)
- ✅ More land-efficient (lower GCR for same power)

**Disadvantages**:
- ❌ Mechanical complexity; maintenance required
- ❌ More expensive (€400-600/kW vs €250-400 for fixed)
- ❌ Moving parts can fail
- ❌ Shadows sweep across land during day (complicated crop adaptation)
- ❌ Control systems require power (small parasitic load ~0.5-1%)

**Best For**:
- Sites with good maintenance infrastructure
- High solar resource areas (>1100 kWh/m²/year)
- Where maximum output critical for financial viability
- Minimal agricultural activity required below

**French Suitability**: Moderate - higher maintenance costs and complexity for modest climate

---

#### E. Sheep/Grazing-Optimized Systems

**Description**: Purpose-designed layouts for pastoral grazing under panels (sheep, goats, cattle)

**Characteristics**:
- **Panel Height**: 1.5-2.5m (for sheep/goat access; cattle need 2.5m+)
- **Row Spacing**: 6-10m (wider for livestock movement)
- **Pasture Management**: Rotational grazing; clipping between rows
- **Ground Coverage**: 25-40%
- **Animal Compatibility**: Sheep graze on grass; can consume vegetation on weeds, reducing maintenance

**Advantages**:
- ✅ Livestock provides "mowing service" (reduced maintenance costs)
- ✅ Grazing improves vegetation diversity (ecosystem benefit)
- ✅ Additional income from wool/milk/meat
- ✅ Natural fertilizer from animal waste (nitrogen cycling)
- ✅ Easier land-use justification (dual-purpose clearly apparent)
- ✅ Excellent for French pastoral regions

**Disadvantages**:
- ❌ Animal management complexity (rotational grazing, veterinary care)
- ❌ Risk of animal damage to panels/wiring (fencing required)
- ❌ Seasonal grazing limitations (winter confinement)
- ❌ Grass/vegetation competes with panel cleaning

**Best For**:
- Pastoral regions (Massif Central, Pyrenees, Jura)
- Existing livestock operations (add solar to existing farm)
- Organic farms (livestock integral to system)
- Multi-year rotation cropping systems

**Case Study**: French Pastoral Farm (Auvergne Region)
- 800 kW solar system with sheep grazing
- 120 sheep graze 30 hectares under/between panels
- Annual returns: €100,000 (electricity) + €60,000 (wool/meat/milk) = €160,000
- Maintenance savings: €15,000/year (no separate mowing required)

---

#### F. Crop-Specific Optimized Systems

**Description**: Panel arrangement and spacing designed specifically for shade-tolerant crop production

**Characteristics**:
- **Panel Spacing**: Optimized for crop light requirements
- **Panel Height**: 1.5-3m (crop-dependent; 3m+ for vegetables)
- **Ground Coverage Ratio**: 30-50% (higher for crops requiring more space)
- **Orientation**: May be non-standard to optimize crop light exposure
- **Vertical Separation**: Panels may be arranged in terraced layers

**Shade-Tolerant Crops for France**:
- **Vegetables** (40-60% shade tolerance): Lettuce, spinach, arugula, chard, kale, bok choy
- **Herbs** (40-70% shade): Parsley, cilantro, mint, basil (with some sun), chives
- **Mushrooms** (70-90% shade): Oyster, shiitake, lion's mane (controlled environment)
- **Berries** (30-50% shade): Blackcurrant, redcurrant, blueberry (some varieties), raspberry
- **Shade-Tolerant Forage**: Clover, alfalfa (with supplemental sun), various grass species

**Advantages**:
- ✅ Higher value crops often possible
- ✅ Specialized market positioning ("solar-grown" premium)
- ✅ Extended season (shade cooling in summer)
- ✅ Reduced water needs (shade reduces evaporation)
- ✅ Targeted design maximizes yields

**Disadvantages**:
- ❌ Higher management complexity
- ❌ Specialized labor required (not standard row-crop farming)
- ❌ Market development needed for "solar-grown" products
- ❌ Yield reduction from shade (typically 30-60% of full-sun equivalent)

**Best For**:
- High-value vegetable/herb production
- Organic operations (premium markets)
- Agritourism/farm-to-table operations
- Regions near urban markets (short transport distance)

**Economic Model Example** (1 hectare, 100 kW system):
- Lettuce production: 50,000 kg/year @ €0.80/kg = €40,000 (vs €70,000 in full sun)
- Electricity: €15,000/year
- **Total: €55,000/year** (vs €40,000 from either activity alone)

## 2.3 Infrastructure Planning

### Site Infrastructure Requirements

#### A. Roads and Access

**Requirements**:
- Main access road: ≥3.5m wide (construction vehicle access)
- Within-site roads: 3-4m wide minimum (gravel/compacted, not permanent asphalt)
- Turning radius: 20-25m minimum (for concrete pump trucks, crane access)
- Surface bearing capacity: ≥2 tons/m² (equipment weight distribution)

**Cost Estimation** (per kilometer):
- Gravel roads: €20,000-40,000/km
- Low-grade asphalt: €40,000-80,000/km
- Reinforced concrete for heavy traffic: €80,000-150,000/km

**Maintenance**:
- Annual resurfacing of gravel roads: €2,000-5,000/km
- Vegetation management: €500-2,000/km/year

#### B. Electrical Infrastructure

**Main Distribution/Substation Area**:
- Location: Central to array for load balancing
- Size: Typically 50-200 m² (depending on system size)
- Equipment**: Transformers, main disconnect, protection equipment, metering
- Environmental Protection: Paved surface, drainage, bunding for spill containment

**Cable Routing**:
- Underground vs. overhead considerations
  - Underground: Higher cost (€40-80/meter trenching + cable); better aesthetics; less weather exposure
  - Overhead: Lower cost (€10-20/meter + poles); aesthetics concern; environmental impact
- French typical: Mix (overhead main lines, underground near structures)

**Grid Connection Point**:
- Coordination with local DSO (Enedis, EDF regional subsidiaries, SNCF Réseau)
- Distance from site to nearest grid: €50,000-500,000+ (depending on distance/capacity)
- Grid upgrade costs (if needed): €100,000-1,000,000+ (shared with other projects)

**Earthing/Grounding System**:
- Rod depth: ≥2m into soil
- Material: Copper (preferred) or galvanized steel
- Cost: €2,000-5,000 per site

#### C. Water and Drainage

**Panel Washing System** (if mechanized):
- Rainwater collection for first wash (free, requires storage)
- Municipal water supply or borehole for regular cleaning
- Recirculation systems: Reduce water use 50-70%
- Cost: €5,000-50,000 (depending on automation level)

**Stormwater Management**:
- Panels reduce rainfall infiltration (increase runoff)
- Mitigations:
  - Permeable surfaces between rows
  - Swales/bioretention areas for runoff capture
  - Retention ponds (if slope >10%)
- French Requirement: Respect local SDAGE (water basin plans) and local drainage rules

**Irrigation** (if agricultural use requires):
- Drip systems under panels: €3,000-8,000 per hectare
- Stored rainwater beneficial (reduce irrigation need 30-50%)
- Greywater reuse: Compliant with French regulations in agricultural context

#### C. Security and Fencing

**Perimeter Fencing**:
- Standard agricultural fencing: €10-20/meter (deer/wildlife)
- Security fencing (if theft concern): €30-80/meter (chain-link, steel pales)
- Gates: €2,000-10,000 per gate (manual or motorized)

**Security Systems**:
- Perimeter cameras: €1,000-5,000
- Motion sensors + lighting: €2,000-8,000
- Remote monitoring: €500-2,000/year (service contract)

**French Considerations**:
- Right to farm (droit de cultiver) - fencing must not impede agricultural access
- Wildlife corridors must be maintained (environmental law)
- Privacy considerations for neighboring properties

#### D. Buildings and Structures

**Central Operations/Monitoring Building** (Optional for larger sites):
- Size: 50-200 m² (depending on need)
- Functions: Office, equipment storage, staff facilities
- Cost: €50,000-200,000 (standard construction)
- Utilities: Electricity (from solar array), water, wastewater

**Equipment Storage Sheds**:
- Minimum: 20-50 m² for tools, spare parts, agrochemicals
- Weather protection critical
- Cost: €5,000-20,000 per shed

**Animal Shelter** (if livestock integration):
- Moveable structures preferred (facilitate rotation grazing)
- Cost: €5,000-30,000 depending on animal type/numbers

#### E. Environmental Features and Biodiversity Infrastructure

**Habitat Creation**:
- Hedgerow planting along edges: €10-30/meter (saplings + maintenance)
- Wildflower strips: €500-2,000/hectare (seed + labor)
- Pollinator nesting structures: €100-500 each (bee boxes, insect hotels)

**Water Features**:
- Ponds for amphibians/birds: €5,000-30,000 (size-dependent)
- Wetland areas: €500-2,000/100 m²

**Benefits**:
- Regulatory compliance (biodiversity requirements)
- Marketing value (environmental positioning)
- Pollination benefits for crops
- Potential certification points (organic, environmental)

### Site Layout Design Principles

#### 1. **Orientation and Solar Geometry**

**France Solar Angles**:
- Latitude range: 42°N (Mediterranean) to 51°N (Nord region)
- Optimal tilt angle: Latitude - 15° to Latitude + 5° for year-round operation
  - Northern France (51°N): 35-40° tilt
  - Central France (47°N): 32-35° tilt
  - Southern France (43°N): 28-32° tilt
  
**Seasonal Optimization**:
- Fixed systems: Compromise angle for year-round
- Seasonal adjustment: Two positions per year (+5° improvement possible)
- Full tracking: East-west tracking +20-35%; north-south tracking +10-15% (rarely cost-justified)

#### 2. **Spacing and Shading**

**Row Spacing Calculation**:

```
Minimum spacing = Panel height / tan(minimum sun angle)

Example for Central France (47°N):
- Winter minimum sun angle: 20°
- Panel height: 3m
- Minimum spacing: 3 / tan(20°) = 8.2m
- Practical spacing: 8-10m (allows equipment passage)
```

**Shading Loss Estimation**:
- Between rows: 5-15% loss (winter sun lower angle)
- Use solar calculation software (PVGIS, PVsyst, HOMER) for precise values
- Agrivoltaic systems typically accept 5-10% shading loss for agricultural benefits

#### 3. **Machinery Access Routes**

**Critical Planning**:
- Establish permanent "field access roads" between panel rows
- Width requirements:
  - Tractors (standard): 3m minimum
  - Combines/harvesters: 4-6m (wider equipment)
  - Livestock movement: 5-8m (for herds)
  
**Turning Areas**:
- T-turns or U-turns: 30-50m² dedicated areas every 500m
- Location: Marked clearly to avoid panel placement

**Height Clearance**:
- Machinery height: Typical 3-4m (check specific equipment)
- Panel clearance: Minimum 0.5m above max machinery height

#### 4. **Utility Corridor Design**

**Electrical/Water/Access Overlay**:
- Combined corridors reduce land fragmentation
- Typical width: 10-15m (cables, pipes, vehicle access)
- Spacing: Every 300-500m along array (depending on array size)

#### 5. **Regulatory Buffers and Setbacks**

**French Typical Requirements**:
- Property line setback: 1-3m (local variation)
- Watercourse buffer: 5-10m (environmental protection)
- Forest edge buffer: 10-20m (fire risk, sunlight access)
- Building setback: 5-20m (visual impact, safety)
- Road visibility: 50m+ triangle (traffic safety)

- **Helioscope**: String-level design with shading analysis - $1,500/year

**Agricultural Planning**:
- **QGIS**: Free GIS software for site mapping
- **Google Earth Pro**: Aerial imagery analysis
- **Agroclimate databases**: Météo-France data portal

**Structural Analysis**:
- **ANSYS/SAP2000**: Complex wind/load analysis (engineering firms use)
- **EnerGIS**: Simplified structural assessment

- [ ] **Inverter Capacity Sized**: String, micro, or hybrid selection?
- [ ] **Electrical Layout Planned**: Cable routing, substation location?
- [ ] **Water Management Designed**: Runoff capture, irrigation plan?
- [ ] **Access Routes Established**: Permanent roads vs. field routes?
- [ ] **Biodiversity Enhancements Planned**: Hedgerows, wetlands, habitat?
- [ ] **Regulatory Setbacks Verified**: All local requirements met?
- [ ] **Environmental Impact Minimized**: Dust, runoff, construction sequencing?
